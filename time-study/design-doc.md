## 0a. Focus lens: time rebound, with felt time pressure as its partner

Decision 2026-10-27. The study centres on time rebound: a tool cuts the time per task, and people spend the saving on more of the task, higher standards, longer distance or new tasks. Felt time pressure is the partner topic, because it is where rebound shows up in daily life. AI stays as one example, not the subject. Research: `research/14-focus-topics.md`, `15-time-rebound.md`, `16-felt-time-pressure.md`.

| Case | Saved | Spent on | Net | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travel | Faster trips | Longer distance | Flat, about 1 hour a day for decades. UK about 58 minutes a day in 2023 | Verified |
| New roads | Shorter trips at first | About 1 to 1 more driving per lane km | Congestion returns | Verified |
| Housework | Appliances | Higher standards, more laundry | Women down about 6 hrs a week 1900 to 1965. Per person slightly up over the century | Verified |
| Meetings | No travel to meet | More and longer calls | Weekly meeting time up 252% since Feb 2020 | Verified |
| AI coding, one example | Expected 24% faster | Prompting, waiting, review | 19% slower on real tasks | Verified |
| Email | Seconds per message | Many more messages | About 28% of the week | Plausible, not on page |

Felt time pressure.

| Measure | Then | Now | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employed Americans with never enough time (Gallup) | 70% in 2011 | 80% in 2018 | Verified |
| Focus on one screen (Gloria Mark, field studies) | 150 s in 2003 | 47 s about 2020 | Verified, small samples |

Against the story. US diary free time rose from 1965 to 1985, and "always rushed" fell between 2004 and 2010 (UMD). The Gallup and Pew items ask different questions, so they cannot be joined into one trend. What it means: rebound is well measured for travel, roads, housework and meetings. Felt pressure is rising on some measures and flat on others.

## 1. Summary verdict

**AI will save time long before it frees time.**

- Measured AI time saving today is small. US users save 5.4% of their hours, which is about 1.4% to 1.6% of all work hours. Danish workers save about 3% with no effect on pay or hours.
- Saved time goes to the employer first, as output. It reaches the worker only by agreement, the home only after robots and infrastructure, and felt time last.
- History agrees. Paid hours roughly halved since 1870, yet weekly leisure grew only 4 to 5 hours. Most free time came as longer schooling, longer lives and retirement.
- The only lever with real evidence that turns saved time into free time is a fixed cut in hours (Iceland, UK four-day trials).
- For most of humanity the largest time burdens are unpaid care, water, fuel and heat. Office AI barely touches them.

US annual hours per worker (OWID, Penn World Table series): about 3,100 in 1870, about 1,915 in 1971 and about 1,789 in 2023. That is a fall of about 7% since 1971, while weekly hours for full-time workers stayed near 40.

Share of adult waking life spent in paid work (Estimate, our own arithmetic): about 50% in 1870, 25% to 30% in 1950, 12% to 15% in 2026.

Five patterns:

1. Paid work fell by about half, then flattened after about 1980.
2. Appliances cut drudgery but standards rose. Home production today is slightly higher than in the early 1900s.
3. Travel time stays near one hour a day. Faster transport bought distance, not time.
4. Weekly leisure per person grew only 4 to 5 hours from 1900 to 2005. Most of the fall in work hours went into schooling.
5. Saved time went to screens. Internet users now spend about 6 hours 38 minutes a day online.

Global note. World annual hours are about 1,900 to 2,100 (Plausible). Poor countries still work 2,200 plus. No long-run non-Western series was found.

## 3. Why saved time rarely becomes free time

| Paradox | One-line idea | Evidence status | What it predicts for AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keynes 1930 | 15-hour week by 2030 | Verified forecast. Income came true, hours did not | Gains show up as output and new goods first |
| Jevons | Cheaper use raises total use | Verified idea. Rebound usually partial, 10% to 60% | More drafts, reports and code, not less time on them |
| Parkinson | Work fills the time available | Verified essay, satirical | Saved time absorbed by polish and review |
| Baumol | Slow-growth sectors grow their share | Verified | People move into care and teaching that do not speed up |
| Linder | Higher hourly value makes leisure feel rushed | Plausible | People feel more pressed, not less |
| Solow and the J-curve | Gains lag while firms reorganise | Verified quote, Plausible model | Years of learning and rework before payback |
| Time poverty | Buying time raises wellbeing | Verified (PNAS 2017) | Helps only if people spend it on rest |
| Schor | Firms prefer long hours | Plausible, contested by time diaries | Firms keep hours steady and take output |
| Hedonic adaptation | Gains become the new baseline | Plausible | Targets reset upward |
| Message load | Cheaper messages mean more messages | Verified, about 275 interruptions a day | AI-written messages raise volume |
| Four-day week | Fixing hours first keeps output | Verified. UK: 56 of 61 firms kept going, quits down 57%. Iceland: 86% now on shorter hours or has the right | The one proven route from saving to free time |

**What this means for Ciklum (Estimate):**

1. Time-and-materials engineering and contact centre work deflate first. Pyramid revenue is the main risk.
2. Move legacy work to fixed price and outcome contracts before clients force a 10% to 15% giveback at renewal.
3. Sell agent build and run, evaluation, and managed AI operations as products.
4. Price on cost per finished task, not cost per token. Budget review and rework hours in every bid.
5. Junior hiring is where the first hit lands. Redesign the junior path rather than cut it.
6. Offer clients a written split of saved hours (output, staff hours, quality). It answers the union and wellbeing question up front.

## 11b. Checking cost and the break-even formula

**Net saved = G x s minus R minus p x F**

G is the time to do the task by hand. s is the share of that time AI removes. R is extra review time. p is the chance an error slips through or needs a redo. F is the time to find and fix it. Break even when G x s equals R plus p x F.

Worked cases, G = 60 minutes. **Illustrative.** The inputs are assumptions set to match measured outcomes, not measurements.

| Case (illustrative) | s | R | p | F | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inside frontier, simple task | 0.25 | 5 min | 0.05 | 30 min | +8.5 min (about +14%) |
| Mature codebase, expert user | 0.30 | 18 min | 0.20 | 45 min | minus 9 min (about 15% slower, near METR 19%) |
| Outside frontier | 0.25 | 10 min | 0.40 | 60 min | minus 19 min |

Measured anchors: METR 19% slower on early 2025 tools, BCG 25.1% faster inside the frontier and 19 points less correct outside, DORA 2024 stability down 7.2% per 25% more adoption, GitClear cloned lines up from 8.3% to 12.3%. Rule of thumb: the saving survives only when review is cheap and errors are rare. The page simulator uses this formula.

## 13. How decisions were made

| Criterion | What we did | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence quality | Measured outcomes over self-report over vendor claims | Self-report ran about 40 points ahead of measured result in the METR trial. Vendors sell the tool |
| Source independence | Vendor items tagged and down-weighted | A party that profits from a number is not a neutral witness |
| Forecast track record | Sources with checkable past calls weighted up, brand alone counts as no evidence | Past headline job-loss calls overstated loss |
| Base rates from history | Electricity, internet and smartphone adoption used as anchors | Institutions changed over decades every time before |
| Triangulation | A claim needs 2 plus sources for Verified | Single sources carry more error and recycled headlines |
| Reversibility | Advice favours steps a firm can undo (pilots, written hour splits) | Wrong bets on irreversible cuts, like junior hiring, are costly to unwind |
| Who bears the cost | Every saving split by employer, worker, home and felt time | Averages hide that the cost lands on juniors, women and the Global South |

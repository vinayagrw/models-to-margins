# Power Takes the Decade, Ageing the Century

Set against each other, the four non-policy forces pull the saved hour in different directions, and the one that wins changes with the horizon. For the next fifteen years the Power force dominates. Productivity gains are flowing to capital, with the US labour share at a record low of **52.8 percent in the second quarter of 2026** (US, Verified), software sets pace and hours for a growing share of workers (one in five EU employees monitored, one in ten with hours set by computer, EU 2024, Verified), platform work grows at double digits, and rich countries are closing the migration routes that staffed care. So paid or paid-equivalent hours per adult rise and free hours fall for people without capital, reaching **strength 3 at 10 and 15 years** on the IMF's modelled 5.5 point fall in the labour share (Scenario) and on the 2040 endpoints from the WTO and from the Japanese and English care gaps (Projection). From 25 years the People force takes over, and it is the only strength 3 force with an agency projection behind it: the UN medium variant puts Korea at **76 people over 65 per 100 of working age in 2050 and 105 in 2075**, Japan at 73 and 72, China at 52 and 84, the US at 38 and 46 (Projection), so paid hours per adult fall mechanically as retirees grow and care lands on the adults in the middle. Place is weak at every horizon (strength 1 to 2) because total travel sits near one hour a day in every rich country with a long series and remote work has been flat at 26 percent of US paid days since 2022, so its long-run effect is to make the travel hour usable rather than to remove it. Culture pushes paid hours down and free hours up at every horizon, but weakly until the 2040s, because only about one in five Americans would take a pay cut for a four-day week and screens already take about 62 percent of American free time. The written rule reinforces rather than offsets: the only hours laws in force are pension ages (France 64 by 2030, Germany 67 by 2031, China 63 for men by about 2040), which push paid hours per worker up, so at 3 to 15 years law and Power point the same way, and at 25 to 50 years law holds per-worker hours up while ageing pulls per-adult hours down. The net reading is paid hours per adult flat to 2041 and falling after, free hours per adult down from about 2029 to 2041 and a wash after 2050, with the long-run sign resting on two unproven scenarios, home automation and busyness losing its status value. No source projects weekly travel hours or home and care hours per adult for 2036, 2051 or 2076. The baselines are 6.9 to 7.0 hours a week of travel and 17.4 to 17.6 hours a week of household and care work per adult.

## Power pushes hardest for fifteen years, and it pushes against free time

The Power force is the strongest of the four in the 2029 to 2041 window because its near-term numbers are measured, not modelled, and its medium-term numbers all land in one place. The US nonfarm business labour share was **52.8 percent in Q2 2026, the lowest since the series began in 1947** at 65.5 percent, against a post-war norm of 60 to 66 percent, and the fall of about ten points since 1980 came mostly after 2000 (US, Verified) ([BLS TED, September 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/labor-share-at-its-lowest-level-52-8-percent-in-second-quarter-2026.htm)). Europe looks different. The UK share rose from 53.8 percent in 1996 to 59.5 percent in 2023, mostly through a 340 percent rise in employers' social contributions rather than pay (UK, Verified) ([ONS, November 2024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/economicoutputandproductivity/output/articles/trendsintheuklabourshare1997to2023/2024-11-25)), and the EU wage share of GDP rose 1.9 points over twenty years to 2025 (EU, Verified) ([Eurostat, March 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Annual_national_accounts_-_evolution_of_the_income_components_of_GDP)). The US is therefore the economy where the "who owns the gains" question bites hardest, and it is also the economy where AI capital is being built: Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta were set to spend **nearly 400 billion dollars on capital expenditure in 2025**, with nearly 100,000 workers laid off at those firms since 2022 (US, Verified) ([WSJ via Dow Jones, July 2025](https://www-web.itiger.com/news/2556865096)). A lower labour share matters for hours because it removes the historical mechanism by which productivity became free time. If workers do not receive the gain as income, they have no slack to buy time with.

Every macro model opened points the same way on distribution. The IMF's 2024 note calibrates a **5.5 percentage point fall in the labour share after AI is introduced**, the same shift in all three of its scenarios, and finds that about 60 percent of advanced economy employment is exposed, with "in most scenarios, AI will likely worsen overall inequality" (global model, Verified source, Scenario) ([IMF SDN 2024/001](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/Staff-Discussion-Notes/Issues/2024/01/14/Gen-AI-Artificial-Intelligence-and-the-Future-of-Work-542379)). Acemoglu's bound puts the productivity prize at **no more than 0.66 percent of total factor productivity over ten years**, and still predicts AI will "widen the gap between capital and labor income" (US, 2024, Projection) ([Acemoglu, NBER w32487](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487)). The WTO projects AI lifting global trade 34 to 37 percent and GDP 12 to 13 percent by 2040, with digitally deliverable services up nearly 40 percent, the largest sectoral gain, and high-income economies gaining about 14 percent against 8 percent for low-income ones (global, 2040, Projection) ([WTO World Trade Report 2025](https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/booksp_e/wtr25_e.pdf)). The models disagree on the size of the prize by a factor of twenty and agree on who gets it. The first measured labour market signal fits: employment of US workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed occupations stood **19 percent below where it would be** relative to less-exposed peers by June 2026, with no comparable gap for experienced workers (US, Verified) ([Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, August 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine)). Euro area youth employment in ICT fell 18.6 percent between Q1 2023 and Q1 2026, though the ECB says the evidence "does not yet allow this pattern to be attributed to generative AI" (euro area, Verified) ([ECB Economic Bulletin 5/2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202605_04~faa9ef5955.en.html)). The force is asymmetric. It leaves owners of capital and experienced professionals alone and lands on entry-level knowledge workers, platform workers and family carers.

Algorithmic management changes the hour from the inside. The 2024 European Working Conditions Survey finds **nearly 20 percent of EU employees monitored by a computer programme, 18 percent allocated tasks by one and 10 percent with working hours set by one**, with Denmark highest on task allocation at 38 percent and Belgium highest on monitoring at 31 percent, and those under algorithmic management report higher job demands and more job strain (EU, 2024, Verified) ([Eurofound, October 2026](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/eurofound/pressreleases/big-brother-reborn-one-in-five-european-employees-digitally-monitored-as-algorithmic-management-takes-hold-3475189)). The measured cost of pace is injuries. Amazon's own OSHA filings show a serious injury rate of **5.9 per 100 workers in 2024, almost double the 3.0 at other warehouses**, with 1,032 serious injuries in Prime Day week, a 35 percent jump on the week before (US, Verified) ([Strategic Organizing Center, May 2025](https://thesoc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/May-2025_Strategic-Organizing-Center_Report-x-Failure-To-Deliver.pdf)). Outside paid hours the same software creates unpaid time: UK gig riders and drivers spent **about ten hours a week waiting unpaid for jobs on the app**, at an average 8 pounds an hour, below the 2022 minimum wage (UK, 2022, Verified) ([University of Cambridge, June 2025](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1085551)). Platform work itself is small in rich countries and growing fast: 3.0 percent of EU adults in 2022 (EU, Verified) ([Eurostat, July 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20240718-1)), 883,000 in Korea in 2023, up 11 percent in a year, with IT services up 141 percent and delivery down 5.5 percent, so platform-style management is spreading into knowledge work (Korea, Verified) ([Asia Economy, August 2024](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2024080514334308819)), and 84 million people, 21 percent of the workforce, in China's new forms of employment (China, Verified) ([China Daily, March 2024](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202403/04/WS65e4fc48a31082fc043ba4a2_2.html)). The EU Platform Work Directive, in force since 1 December 2024 with a transposition deadline of 2 December 2026, is the first binding law on algorithmic management, and France has said it will miss the deadline (EU, Verified for the directive, Plausible for the delay) ([European Parliament, November 2024](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1794723)). Countervailing power arrives two to five years after the exposure.

The channel that reaches free hours most directly is care staffing. Foreign-born workers were 21 percent of long-term care staff across 26 European OECD countries in 2024, up from 14 percent in 2014 (Europe, Plausible) ([OECD Health at a Glance 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-2025_8f9e3f98-en/full-report/long-term-care-workers_9c3bdbaf.html)), and **28 percent of the 5.4 million US direct care workers are immigrants**, in a sector projected to need 9.7 million job openings between 2024 and 2034 (US, Verified) ([PHI, November 2025](https://www.phinational.org/?p=22328)). England is the natural experiment. The care worker visa route closed to new overseas applicants on 22 July 2025, international recruits fell from 105,000 in 2023/24 to 50,000 in 2024/25 and 30,000 in 2025/26, posts filled by British nationals fell 40,000 in a year, and the sector still needs **410,000 to 470,000 more posts by 2040**, roughly 27,000 a year (England, Plausible for the recruit series, Verified in trade press for the 470,000) ([HFMA, June 2026](https://www.hfma.org.uk/news-alerts/further-fall-international-recruits-social-care), [The MJ, October 2025](https://www.themj.co.uk/domestic-recruitment-retention-challenges-persist-adult-social-care)). Japan had about 54,000 foreign care workers under the Specified Skilled Worker status in June 2025 against a government estimate of a **570,000 care worker shortage by fiscal 2040** (Japan, Verified) ([The Star, December 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/12/14/japan-looks-to-india-for-caregivers-as-domestic-labour-crunch-deepens)), and Korea's Filipino caregiver pilot had 98 workers serving 185 households in January 2025 with 17 of 19 local governments uninterested in joining (Korea, Verified) ([Korea Times, January 2025](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/society/20250115/seoul-hails-philippine-caregiver-pilot-program-success-yet-nationwide-expansion-remains-uncertain)). Every paid care hour that is not staffed becomes an unpaid family hour or an unmet need, and that conversion grows with ageing in every country in scope.

The horizon calls follow from those numbers. At 1 year the force is weak (strength 1): the labour share is at a record low but no hours series has moved, so paid hours are flat and free hours slightly down on the care margin (US, 2026, Verified). At 3 years it reaches strength 2 on the EWCS monitoring and scheduling shares, with paid hours up for low earners and carers, down for entry-level knowledge workers, and free hours down (EU, 2024, Verified). At 5 years it stays at 2, anchored on England's care recruits falling to 30,000 against 27,000 new posts needed a year (England, 2025/26, Plausible). At 10 years it reaches **strength 3** on the IMF's 5.5 point shift, because if the labour share falls that far workers must hold hours to hold income and the 19 percent entry-level gap becomes a cohort effect (global model, Scenario). At 15 years it stays at 3 because every verified 2040 endpoint lands there, the WTO's trade and services numbers and Japan's 570,000 gap (Projection). At 25 years it drops to 2 with a wide range, since no verified 2050 figure exists for the labour share, platform work or care migration and the nearest anchor is the English 2040 care gap (Scenario). At 50 years the direction depends on who owns AI capital. The only long anchor is historical: the US labour share fell 13 points over 79 years and hours fell most when the share was high (US, Verified), so the trend-continuation case is paid hours up and free hours down for those without capital, and the shared-gains case reverses it (Scenario, strength 2).

## Ageing is the slowest force, the most certain, and the one that rules after 2050

The People force moves half a point a year and cannot be stopped before 2050, because the people who will be 65 then are already 40. The UN World Population Prospects 2024 medium variant gives these old age dependency ratios, people aged 65 and over per 100 aged 15 to 64 (Verified estimates to 2023, Projection after) ([Our World in Data, UN WPP 2024](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/age-dependency-ratio-old), [PopulationPyramid.net, UN WPP 2024](https://www.populationpyramid.net/japan/2075/)).

| Country | 1965 | 2025 | 2050 Projection | 2075 Projection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | 15.4 | 28.5 | 37.9 | 45.7 |
| United Kingdom | 19.0 | 31.1 | 40.9 | 49.2 |
| France | 19.4 | 36.8 | 48.5 | 50.6 |
| Germany | 19.3 | 38.0 | 53.5 | 52.2 |
| Sweden | 19.1 | 33.5 | 42.9 | 52.8 |
| Japan | 9.2 | 51.0 | 73.1 | 71.8 |
| Korea | 6.1 | 29.3 | 75.6 | 105.3 |
| China | 6.6 | 21.4 | 52.3 | 83.8 |
| World | 8.9 | 16.0 | 25.8 | 34.2 |

The arithmetic is mechanical. Because retired people count as adults, a 65 and over share moving from **18.4 percent to 26.7 percent in the US between 2025 and 2075** is by itself an 8 point shift of the adult population out of paid work, before any working age person changes their hours (US, Projection) ([PopulationPyramid.net](https://www.populationpyramid.net/china/2075/)). Korea moves from 20.3 to 47.4 percent and by 2075 has more people over 65 than of working age. The US Census Bureau's middle series has people 65 and over outnumbering children under 18 from 2029 (US, Projection) ([US Census Bureau, November 2023](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023/population-projections.html)), and the UN expects people aged 80 and over to outnumber infants by the mid 2030s, reaching 265 million worldwide (world, Projection) ([UN DESA, WPP 2024 key messages](https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/key_messages_wpp_2024_20240709.pdf)). Germany, Japan and France plateau between 2050 and 2075 because their baby boom cohorts have died, while Korea, China, the UK, Sweden and the US keep rising, so the 50 year horizon is a Korea and China story more than a European one.

What ageing does to unpaid time is the harder question, and so far the answer is almost nothing, which is itself the finding. US adults spent **2.52 hours a day on household activities and caring for household members in 2024 (17.6 hours a week), against 2.38 hours in 2003 (16.7 hours)**, a rise of under one hour a week in 21 years while the 65 and over share rose from 12 to 18 percent (US, Verified) ([BLS ATUS 2003](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/history/atus_09142004.txt), [BLS ATUS 2024](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06262025.htm)). Child care falling with fertility offset elder care rising. That offset is ending. Fertility is already low everywhere and the UN medium variant keeps it within 0.1 to 0.3 of today's level through 2075 everywhere except Korea and China, where it rises from 0.72 and 1.00 births per woman to 1.19 and 1.28 (Projection) ([Our World in Data, UN WPP 2024](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/fertility-rate-with-projections)), so there is little child care left to shed. Meanwhile fewer children has not released parental time: across 11 Western countries mothers' daily child care rose from about 54 minutes in 1965 to 104 in 2012 and fathers' from 16 to 59 while fertility halved (Verified) ([Dotti Sani and Treas 2016](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/handle/2434/625906/1171156/JMF%20DottiSani%20Treas%202016%20pre-print.pdf)). The gender split is where the load lands. The ILO counted **16.4 billion hours a day of unpaid care work worldwide, 76.2 percent done by women**, and found the gap narrowing by only 7 minutes a day over two decades in 23 countries (world, Verified) ([ILO, 2018](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-calls-urgent-action-prevent-looming-global-care-crisis)). In 2023 **708 million women and 40 million men** were outside the labour force because of unpaid care (world, Verified) ([ILO, October 2024](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/unpaid-care-work-prevents-708-million-women-participating-labour-market)). The US housework gap fell from 25 hours a week in 1965 to 10 in 1985 and about 6 by 2010, so three quarters of the closing happened in the first twenty years and the pace since is about 1.5 hours a week per decade, almost all from men adding hours (US, Verified) ([Bianchi et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242525/)). Japanese men did 51 minutes a day of housework and related work in 2021 against 3 hours 24 minutes for women (Japan, Verified) ([Statistics Bureau of Japan, 2021](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/shakai/2021/pdf/timeuse-a2021.pdf)). At the post-1985 US pace the remaining gap closes around 2065. At the ILO's 23 country pace it does not close within the horizon.

No agency projects unpaid care hours, so the forward anchors are paid care demand. Japan's health ministry needs care workers to rise from about **2.15 million in FY2022 to 2.40 million in FY2026 and 2.72 million in FY2040** (Japan, Projection) ([MHLW, July 2024](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/newpage_02977.html)). England needs around 470,000 extra adult social care posts by 2040, a 27 percent rise (England, Projection, Verified in trade press) ([The MJ, October 2025](https://www.themj.co.uk/domestic-recruitment-retention-challenges-persist-adult-social-care)). The US direct care workforce grew from 3.5 million in 2014 to 5.4 million in 2024 and is projected to add **772,000 jobs and 9.7 million openings between 2024 and 2034**, more than any other occupation (US, Projection) ([PHI Key Facts 2025](https://www.phinational.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PHI-DCW-Key-Facts-Report-2025.pdf)). The OECD estimates long-term care workers must rise 60 percent by 2040 just to hold today's ratio (OECD, Plausible) ([Global Coalition on Aging, 2022](https://globalcoalitiononaging.com/2022/03/29/women-work-wellness-and-that-aging-thing/)). The nearest proxies for unpaid care are the UK's full-time unpaid carers rising from 1.1 million in 2003/04 to 1.9 million in 2023/24 (UK, Verified) ([IPPR, September 2025](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/revealed-number-of-full-time-unpaid-carers-soars-by-70-per-cent)) and US family caregivers rising 20 million in ten years to 63 million (US, Verified) ([AARP, July 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)), both faster than population growth.

Two smaller People elements pull in opposite directions. Sleep is the one block of the day that has grown in every country with a recent series: **US diary sleep rose from 8.57 hours a day in 2003 to 9.04 in 2024**, 28 minutes, in every age group (US, Verified) ([BLS ATUS 2024](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06262025.htm), [USAFacts, January 2026](https://usafacts.org/articles/are-americans-sleeping-more-or-less/)), Japan's rose 14 minutes to 7 hours 54 between 2016 and 2021 after decades of decline (Japan, Verified) ([Statistics Bureau of Japan, 2021](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/shakai/2021/pdf/timeuse-a2021.pdf)), and the UK's rose 16 minutes to 9 hours 3 between 2020 and 2023 (UK, Verified) ([ONS Time use in the UK](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/bulletins/timeuseintheuk/latest)). Ageing explains part of it (older Americans sleep 9 minutes more) and remote work part (full-time workers sleep 44 minutes less than non-workers). Sleep takes 10 to 15 minutes a day per decade from the sum of paid and free time and nothing suggests it reverses. Machines at home are the only element pushing free hours up, and the measured base is tiny. Sixty-five AI experts polled in 2023 expected **39 percent of time on domestic work to be automatable within ten years**, 44 percent for housework and only 28 percent for care, with physical child care least at 21 percent (UK and Japan, Verified, expert Scenario) ([Lehdonvirta et al., PLOS ONE 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0281282)). The one measured trial, ten Oxford households, halved human floor cleaning time from 3 hours 43 minutes to 1 hour 50 across matched four-week periods while the floor got cleaned about three times as often (UK, Verified) ([Oxford Research Archive](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:607b7859-b18d-4351-bd18-cb6a6fdcc04c/files/s5m60qv36w)). Consumer robot sales were about 20 million units in 2024 and 34.2 million in 2025 (global, Verified) ([IFR, October 2025](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/service-robots-see-global-growth-boom), [IFR, 2026](https://ifr.org/news/global-sales-of-professional-service-robots-surge-24-percent/)), and about one in ten UK homes owns a cleaning robot (UK, Plausible) ([European Cleaning Journal, 2025](https://www.europeancleaningjournal.com/magazine/articles/latest-news/household-robots-are-booming-thanks-to-the-youth-market)). Scaled to the population that is 3 to 12 minutes a week per adult today. If the 39 percent scenario landed by 2033 it would return about 6 hours a week per adult, roughly enough to offset the care rise, which is why the free hours sign for People after 2040 is less certain than the paid hours sign.

The horizon calls: flat and strength 0 at 1 year, because the dependency ratio moves half a point a year and unpaid work per US adult moved 0.4 minutes a day per year over 2003 to 2024, invisible against survey noise (US, Verified). Paid down at strength 1 from 3 years, on the 2029 Census crossing (US, Projection), and free down from 5 years on Japan's 2.40 million care workers needed by FY2026 (Japan, Projection). Both down at strength 2 at 10 and 15 years on the US 9.7 million care openings and Japan's 2.72 million by FY2040 (Projection). Both down at **strength 3 at 25 years in Korea, Japan, Germany and China** and 2 in the US, UK, France and the Nordics, on the 2050 ratios (Projection). Both down at strength 3 at 50 years on the 2075 ratios, with the free hours cell carrying wide uncertainty because fewer siblings mean each adult carer serves more older relatives while a fully automated home has no measured base (Projection for the ratios, Scenario for the offset).

## Place changes what the travel hour is for, not how much of it there is

Total travel time per person sits close to one hour a day in every rich country with a long series and has moved by single-digit minutes per decade. Summing the ten travel rows of the American Time Use Survey gives **1.17 hours a day in 2019, 1.04 in 2024 and 0.99 in 2025**, so 6.9 hours a week per adult, with work travel averaged over all adults falling from 17 to 13 minutes a day as the share doing any work travel fell from 35.5 to 29.0 percent (US, Verified rows, derived sum) ([BLS ATUS Table A-1 2019](https://www.bls.gov/tus/tables/a1-2019.pdf), [2025](https://www.bls.gov/tus/tables/a1-2025.pdf)). England residents travelled **362 hours in 2024, about an hour a day, against 370 in 2019**, while commuting trips fell about a fifth, which fits the travel budget idea that time freed from commuting went to other trips (England, Verified) ([DfT NTS 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024/nts-2024-introduction-and-main-findings)). France sat at 62 minutes in 2019, Norway at 71, Germany at 84 in 2023 and Korea near 108, the longest of 43 countries (Plausible) ([MiD 2023](https://www.mobilitaet-in-deutschland.de/pdf/MiD2023_Studienzusammenfassung.pdf), [Asia News Network, November 2025](https://asianews.network/south-korea-logs-worlds-longest-commute-which-studies-say-may-fuel-loneliness/)). The review literature puts the regularity at about 70 minutes plus or minus 10 across four decades and says its cause is still unknown (Plausible) ([Ahmed and Stopher 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/transr/v34y2014i5p607-625.html)). Dense metros sit above their national averages, Seoul and Gyeonggi at about 90 minutes of weekday commuting and Beijing at 89 minutes a day (Plausible), so density raises travel time at the city level even when national budgets hold.

Remote work is the one Place change that has already happened, and its dividend is banked. About **26 percent of US paid full days were worked from home in July 2026**, flat since mid 2022, with employer plans at 1.3 to 1.5 days a week matching behaviour, 12 percent of full-time employees fully remote and 26 percent hybrid, and the rate running from 15.6 percent of workdays for the least educated to 31.4 percent for the most (US, Verified) ([WFH Research SWAA, September 2026](https://wfhresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/WFHResearch_updates_September2026.pdf)). Britain had 28 percent of working adults hybrid in early 2025, 41 percent of graduates against 4 percent of those with no qualifications (GB, Verified) ([ONS, June 2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/articles/whohasaccesstohybridworkingreatbritain/latest)), the EU about 8.9 percent usually at home in 2024 (EU, Plausible), Japan under 20 percent and Korea 0.5 days a week, the lowest of 40 countries (Plausible). What the saved commute becomes is the measured number that matters most for this force. Across 27 countries in 2021 to 2022, working from home saved **72 minutes a day on home days, and workers put 40 percent of it back into their jobs**, about 11 percent into caregiving and the rest into leisure, worth about 2 hours a week per worker then and about 1 hour once the pandemic effect faded (27 countries, Verified) ([Aksoy et al., NBER w30866](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30866)). Applied to the US that is about 25 minutes a week of free time per adult, which matches the fall in work travel in the diaries. The remote work effect on paid hours is small and positive, not negative. Saved commute time partly becomes extra paid work, so this force nudges paid and free hours up together, funded by travel.

Housing costs push the other way on paid hours, weakly. Half of US renter households, **22.7 million, paid over 30 percent of income on housing in the 2026 report, 2.3 million more than in 2019**, and home prices were up 54 percent since 2020 (US, Plausible) ([NC Housing Coalition on JCHS 2026](https://nchousing.org/2026-state-of-the-nations-housing-report/)). Private renters in England spent 34 percent of income on housing in 2023/24, up from 32 percent in 2019/20 (England, Plausible) ([English Housing Survey 2023 to 2024](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2023-to-2024-headline-findings-on-demographics-and-household-resilience/chapter-2-housing-costs-and-affordability)). US multiple jobholding rose from **5.2 percent of the employed in 2024 to 5.4 percent in 2025**, with a record 9.3 million in November 2025 (US, Verified for the annual shares) ([BLS CPS table 36](https://www.bls.gov/cps/cpsaat36.htm)). That is about 370,000 more people and under 2 minutes a week per worker. The one causal rent study runs the other way, with rent stabilisation lowering tenants' hours by 5.6 a week (US, Plausible) ([HKBU working paper](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/housing-assistance-and-labor-supply-the-case-of-rent-control/)), and owners sitting on price gains work less, so the two channels partly cancel nationally.

Autonomous travel is real and tiny. Waymo went from 50,000 paid rides a week in May 2024 to **500,000 a week in March 2026 across ten US cities with about 3,000 vehicles** (US, Verified, company reported) ([TechCrunch, March 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/27/waymo-skyrocketing-ridership-in-one-chart/)), which is about 70,000 rides a day against roughly a billion US person trips a day, under 0.01 percent. Industry surveys pushed adoption out by one to two years in 2025 to 2026, with Level 4 trucking expected in 2029 and urban pilots for privately owned autonomous cars around 2032 (global, Plausible, Scenario) ([McKinsey survey via eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/158514)). The research on what automation does to the travel hour is consistent: riding instead of driving cuts the felt cost of travel time by 20 to 30 percent (Plausible) ([Lehtonen et al. 2021](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11116-021-10211-6.pdf)), and under a stable budget that shows up as longer trips rather than fewer hours. The ITF projects urban passenger kilometres up 74 percent between 2019 and 2050 and the Department for Transport projects distance driven in England and Wales up 8 to 54 percent between 2025 and 2060 (Plausible, Projection) ([ITF Transport Outlook 2023](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/itf-transport-outlook-2023_b6cc9ad5-en.html), [DfT NRTP 2022](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63975bcfd3bf7f3f7d1cf440/national-road-traffic-projections-2022.pdf)). If half of car time became usable and cars are about 60 percent of travel time, about 2 hours a week per adult becomes usable, 0.8 to work and 1.2 to free time by the 40 percent rule. That is a reclassification of the same hour, not a saving.

The horizon calls: paid up and free flat at strength 1 at 1 and 3 years, on the second-job margin (US, 2025, Verified) and the flat 26 percent remote share (US, 2026, Verified). Paid up and free up at strength 1 at 5 years on robotaxi scale (US, 2026, Verified) and at 10 years on the slipped adoption timelines (Scenario). Strength 2 at 15 years on about 2 hours a week of car time made usable (derived Scenario), at 25 years on the ITF and DfT distance projections (Projection), and at 50 years on the travel budget holding for four decades, so hours stay fixed and use changes (Plausible). Paid hours at 50 years are flat.

## Culture wants the shorter week and will not pay for it yet

The stated preference is large, rising and conditional. Most EU workers, 56 percent, want to keep the hours they work, but the share preferring fewer hours rose from **27 percent in 2015 to 33 percent in 2024** (EU, Verified) ([Eurofound EWCS 2024 release](https://www.pubt.io/view/6EF11E004726B817717EED5DEA69A7989BEACB5D)). In the US **77 percent of workers say a four-day, 40 hour week would improve their wellbeing, 82 percent of 18 to 29 year olds against 69 percent of those over 60** (US, 2023, Verified) ([Gallup, 2023](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/513923/american-workers-optimistic-impact-wellness-initiatives.aspx)), 78 percent of more than 13,000 Korean office workers back a 4.5 day week (Korea, 2025, Verified) ([Asia Economy, December 2025](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025120314352136457)), and 92 percent of about 157,000 Weibo respondents voted against make-up weekends (China, 2023, Verified) ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1012786)). Support collapses when pay is at stake. Roughly **one in five Americans would take a pay cut for a four-day week** across three surveys (US, Plausible) ([ResumeBuilder](https://www.resumebuilder.com/1-in-5-workers-would-take-a-pay-cut-in-exchange-for-a-4-day-work-week/)). The gap between wanting it at the same pay (67 to 78 percent) and accepting less pay (about 20 percent) is the best single measure of how much of this is culture and how much is an income constraint. Culture is the 20 percent. Korea adds a twist: support is highest among those working normal hours and lowest, 71 percent, among those working more than 70 hours, so the people most embedded in long hours defend them.

Experience moves the valuation. In the UK pilot of 61 firms and about 2,900 employees, weekly hours fell from 38 to 34, life satisfaction rose from 6.69 to 7.56 out of 10, satisfaction with time for the things they like rose from 5.28 to 7.51, sick days fell from 2 to 0.7 a month, **90 percent definitely wanted to continue and 15 percent said no amount of money would take them back to five days**, while 46 percent would return for a rise of under 25 percent (UK, 2022, Verified) ([Autonomy, February 2023](https://autonomy.work/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/The-results-are-in-The-UKs-four-day-week-pilot.pdf)). The unmet wish for hobby time fell from 87 to 54 percent and for time with children from 59 to 32 percent, men's child care time rose 27 percent against 13 percent for women, and 68 percent reported no change in the housework split. Portugal's government-backed trial cut hours from 41.6 to 36.5, cut the share struggling to balance work and life from 50 to 16 percent, and found workers valuing the four-day week at **28 percent of salary**, with 93 percent wanting to continue (Portugal, 2024, Verified) ([Henley Business School, July 2024](https://www.henley.ac.uk/news/2024/results-from-portugals-government-backed-4-day-week-trial-released)). Germany's 45 organisation trial kept 73 percent of firms on reduced time and recorded about 13 fewer stress minutes a day on wearables (Germany, 2024, Plausible) ([The Local, November 2024](https://www.thelocal.de/20241106/what-are-the-results-of-germanys-four-day-work-week-trial)). The saved day went to care, rest and hobbies, not paid side work, and the revealed use matches the stated wish. The counter-signal is Gallup's finding that US workers already on four-day weeks report slightly higher burnout than those on five, which likely reflects compressed ten-hour days (US, 2023, Verified).

Busyness still carries status, and the reversal that put it there took forty years. UK diaries show the Veblen relationship between status and work time reversed between 1961 and 2001, so that "work, not leisure, is now the signifier of dominant social status", with professional work rising from 17 to 39 percent of all paid work time (UK, Verified) ([Gershuny, ISER 2005](https://www.iser.essex.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/files/working-papers/iser/2005-09.pdf)). In US experiments, 307 adults rated a busy friend higher in status than a leisurely one, about 12 percent of 1,100 celebrity humblebrags were complaints about overwork, and the effect depends on believing in social mobility: **Italians, scoring 3.98 on perceived mobility against 4.91 for Americans, rated the leisurely life as higher status**, the reverse (US and Italy, 2017, Verified) ([Bellezza, Paharia and Keinan, JCR 2017](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19293/Conspicuous%20Consumption%20of%20Time%20JCR.pdf)). Sixty-two percent of Americans call their week busy and 14 percent extremely busy (US, 2019, Verified) ([Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/node/510191)). The mechanism gives a lever. Where belief in mobility falls, busyness loses its signal value, but no survey has tested whether that is happening. If a flip back toward leisure as status is under way, the Gershuny timescale says it shows up in diaries in the 2040s, not the 2030s.

The free hour that already exists goes to screens, and it has not grown. US leisure was **5.1 hours a day in 2005 and 5.2 in 2025**, television held at 2.6 hours, games and computer use rose from 25 minutes in 2015 to 37, and socialising fell from 41 to 35 minutes with participation down from 38 to 30 percent (US, Verified) ([BLS ATUS 2005](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_07272006.pdf), [BLS ATUS 2025](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm)). Screens take about 62 percent of that leisure on BLS numbers, and more once phone use coded elsewhere is counted. The share of waking time spent alone rose from 43.5 percent in 2003 to 49.7 percent in 2022 and time with people from other households fell from 21.9 to 14.3 percent (US, Verified) ([Atalay 2024](https://enghinatalay.github.io/time_use.pdf)). Chinese residents spent 5 hours 37 minutes a day on the internet in 2024, nearly three hours more than in 2018, while housework fell 28 minutes (China, Verified) ([Global Times, October 2024](https://globaltimes.cn/page/202410/1322190.shtml)). Global social media time has plateaued at 2 hours 21 minutes a day (global, Verified) ([DataReportal Digital 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-global-overview-report)), and **53 percent of Americans say they spend too much time on their phone, 45 percent tried to cut back and only a quarter of those largely succeeded** (US, 2026, Verified) ([Pew, September 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/01/about-half-of-americans-say-they-spend-too-much-time-on-their-smartphone/)). The saved hour of the last two decades did not appear as more free time. It appeared as a shift inside free time from people to screens, and the culture is turning against the screen hour even as it keeps spending it.

The class split means free hours are not one good. Between 1965 and 2005 leisure rose about 8 hours a week for adults without a high school diploma and fell 6 hours for college graduates (US, Verified) ([Aguiar and Hurst, 2009](https://www.markaguiar.com/citation/leisure-inequality)), and in 2005 the least educated had **1.9 more hours a day of leisure than graduates** (US, Verified) ([BLS ATUS 2005](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_07272006.pdf)). Since then the less educated took the extra leisure at home, alone and on screens, so the gap in hours persisted while the gap in sociable leisure reversed (US, Verified) ([Atalay 2024](https://enghinatalay.github.io/time_use.pdf)). The educated say they want more time and have less of it. Felt time pressure has not tracked hours at all: about half of Americans have said they lack enough time in every Gallup reading from 1990 to 2015 (US, Verified) ([Gallup, 2015](https://news.gallup.com/poll/187982/americans-perceived-time-crunch-no-worse-past.aspx)), while global daily workplace stress rose from 31 percent in 2009 to 40 percent in 2025, highest in the US and Canada at 50 percent and East Asia at 46 (global, Verified) ([Gallup State of the Global Workplace 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-global-data.aspx)), the two regions with the highest stated support for shorter weeks.

The horizon calls: paid down and free up at strength 1 at 1 and 3 years, on the 33 percent EU share (EU, 2024, Verified) and the 92 percent of UK pilot firms that stayed on four days (UK, 2023, Verified). Paid down at strength 2 from 5 years, on the EU share reaching about 36 to 37 percent by 2030 if the 2015 to 2024 pace holds (EU, Projection by arithmetic), with free hours still at 1 because they stay screen-bound. Both at 2 at 10 years on cohort replacement (US, 2023, Verified) and at 15 years on the 28 percent of salary and the 15 percent at any price (Portugal 2024, UK 2023, Verified). Still 2 at 25 years because the Gershuny reversal took forty years (Scenario). Strength 3 at 50 years only in the Scenario where busyness loses its signal value as mobility beliefs fall, as it already has in Italy (Scenario, low confidence).

## Set against each other, Power rules to 2041 and People after, and the law sides with both

The written-rule finding from the policy research is specific. Across two centuries hours law bit at the long-hours tail and ratified what wages and bargaining had delivered, with over 80 percent of US economic historians agreeing the pre-Depression fall in hours was "primarily due to economic growth and the increased wages it brought" (US, Verified) ([Whaples, EH.net](https://eh.net/encyclopedia/hours-of-work-in-u-s-history/)). As of November 2026 the only hours laws in force push paid hours up: France's legal retirement age reaches 64 in 2030 (France, Verified) ([La finance pour tous](https://www.lafinancepourtous.com/2023/04/18/reforme-des-retraites-promulgation-de-la-loi-le-14-avril-2023/)), Germany's standard age of 67 completes for the 1964 cohort around 2031 (Germany, Verified) ([dejure.org, SGB VI section 35](https://dejure.org/gesetze/SGB_VI/35.html)), China raises men's retirement age from 60 to 63 over 15 years from 2025 (China, Verified) ([NPC Observer, September 2024](https://npcobserver.com/2024/09/china-npc-raise-retirement-age/)), and Korea's bill for 65 by 2033 was still in committee (Korea, Verified) ([Korea Times, November 2025](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/politics/20251109/debate-heats-up-over-extending-retirement-age-to-65)). No G7 or EU government has legislated a shorter week in response to AI, Spain's 37.5 hour bill lost 178 to 170, and the only quantified 2050 scenario puts hours per worker between **1,600 and 2,070 a year, from 2,100 in 2025** (global, Projection) ([BCG scenarios 2050, April 2026](https://web-assets.bcg.com/20/b1/521bbd2d431fba3b32dac5af5d64/bcg-scenarios-2050-apr-2026-web.pdf)). Three readings come out of the table. First, the dominant force switches once, from Power to People, between 2041 and 2051, and the switch is a switch in evidence quality as much as in strength. Power's strength 3 rests on a model shift with no calendar date and on 2040 projections, while People's strength 3 rests on the UN medium variant, whose 2050 cohorts are already born. Second, paid hours per adult and paid hours per worker move apart. The People force cuts paid hours per adult of all ages because retirees grow as a share, while the Power force and the pension laws raise paid hours per working-age adult, and both are true at once. For working-age adults the 10 to 25 year window is one of rising paid hours and rising care, which is the squeeze on the adults in the middle. Third, Culture is the only force pushing free hours up at every horizon, and it never exceeds Power or People until the 50 year scenario, because its number is a stated preference that one in five would pay for and because the free hour it would create already goes two thirds to screens. The written rule does not change any of these readings. It reinforces Power at 3 to 15 years through pension ages and offsets People at 25 to 50 years in the same way, and nothing on the books or in committee cuts hours.

The uncertainties are concentrated in three cells. The People free hours sign after 2040 depends on whether home automation reaches anything like the 39 percent expert scenario, and the measured base is ten households and one task. The Power direction after 2050 depends on who owns AI capital, and no number exists past 2040 for any of its five sub-forces. The Culture strength 3 at 50 years depends on busyness losing its status value, for which the only evidence is a cross-country experiment with 60 people. Everything else in the table rests on measured series or agency projections.

## Conclusion

The question was which forces beyond policy drive time spent and saved, and the answer reorders the usual debate. The force that most people name first, machines at home and on the road, is the weakest in the evidence: robot vacuums save 3 to 12 minutes a week per adult today and robotaxis carry under 0.01 percent of trips, and both are more likely to raise standards and lengthen journeys than to return hours. The force most people name last, who owns the gain, is the strongest for the next fifteen years, and it points the wrong way for free time. The labour share, the monitoring shares, the youth gap and the care visa numbers are all measured, and they say the saved minute is being kept by whoever owns the system that saved it, exactly as the government AI pilots found. Ageing then takes over not because it is strong in any year but because it is certain in every year, and it reshapes the question from how much free time an adult has to who the adult is caring for.

What this implies for the page is that a single "free hours per adult" number hides the mechanism. The honest display is three groups moving apart: working-age adults without capital, whose paid and care hours both rise to 2041, retirees, whose growing share pulls the average paid hour down after 2050, and the educated, who want the shorter week most and already have the least leisure. Culture is the only force that could turn the free hours sign positive at the long horizon, and the two levers it depends on, experience of shorter weeks and a fall in the status value of being busy, are both testable within a decade. The series to watch are the ones no agency projects: unpaid care hours per adult in the UK, US and Japanese time use surveys, and the share of saved commute time that goes back to the employer.

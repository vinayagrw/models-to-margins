# Do self reported AI time savings show up in measured output, pay or hours at the firm or economy level, and how far does self report overstate the saving?

Scope note. This file adds only what the study does not already hold. The 2025 baselines (St Louis Fed 5.4 percent of user hours, Denmark 3 percent saved with pay and hours null above 1 percent, METR 2025 felt 20 percent faster and measured 19 percent slower, Korea near zero link between time saved and output, UK government trials) sit in design-doc.md, research/03, research/13, research/15 and research/30-policy-04 and are not repeated. Searches run 2 Nov 2026.

## What is known

- US, Q3 2026: self reported time saved by generative AI is 2.08 percent of all work hours (employed adults 18 to 64, non users counted as zero). Q4 2025 1.99, Q1 2026 2.07, Q2 2026 2.17. FRED series updated 20 Oct 2026. Verified. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RPSGENAITSALL
- US, Aug 2026: 40.5 percent of employed adults use generative AI at work, 5.3 percent of all work hours involve it, 2.1 percent of all work hours are saved. Survey estimates from the Real-Time Population Survey (Bick, Blandin, Deming). Verified. https://www.genaiadoptiontracker.com/
- US, 2026: the Bick, Blandin, Deming paper is listed as forthcoming in Management Science. The series remains self reported. No wave converts the saved hours into measured output. Plausible (listing seen in search, journal page not opened). https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4964384
- Denmark, 2026 revision (NBER w33777, revised Mar 2026, Brookings summary 6 Oct 2026): survey of about 25,000 workers at 7,000 workplaces in 11 exposed occupations, linked to monthly pay and hours records to Dec 2024. Null effects on earnings and recorded hours for workers and workplaces, "ruling out average effects larger than 2%" two years after adoption. Verified. https://www.brookings.edu/articles/still-waters-rapid-currents-early-labor-market-transformation-under-generative-ai/
- Denmark, 2026: 98 percent of adopters say chatbots have not changed their pay. 85 percent of users say saved time goes to other tasks. At firms that push chatbots, 19 percent of users report saving more than one hour a day, yet the average across all users and hours is a few percent of work time. Verified. https://www.brookings.edu/articles/still-waters-rapid-currents-early-labor-market-transformation-under-generative-ai/
- Denmark, note on the bound: the 2025 version ruled out pay and hours effects above 1 percent. The 2026 version states 2 percent over a longer two year window. Read the later number as the current one. Verified. https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33777/w33777.pdf
- US, Feb 2026 (METR update, 24 Feb 2026): second developer study from Aug 2025, 57 developers (10 returning, 47 new). Returning developers finished tasks 18 percent faster with AI (interval 38 percent faster to 9 percent slower). New developers 4 percent faster (interval 15 percent faster to 9 percent slower). Both intervals include zero. Verified. https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/
- US, Feb 2026: METR says the new data is "an unreliable signal" because 30 to 50 percent of developers held back tasks they did not want to do without AI. METR believes developers are likely faster now than in early 2025 and treats its estimate as a lower bound. Verified. https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/
- US, May 2026 (METR survey, 349 technical workers, Feb to Apr 2026): median self reported speed change 3 times, median self reported change in value of work 1.4 to 2 times. METR restates that its 2025 RCT found developers overestimated the time effect by 40 percentage points. Convenience sample. Verified. https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/
- US, May 2026: METR warns "survey results are not necessarily grounded in reality" and notes its own staff, who know the past perception gap, reported the lowest gains of any subgroup. Verified. https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/
- US, Nov 2025 revision (Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica, Stanton, NBER w33795): randomised access to Microsoft 365 Copilot across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers for six months. Of treated workers 80 percent used it and spent about two fewer hours a week on email and less time outside normal hours. The authors "do not detect shifts in the quantity or composition of workers' tasks". Vendor note: Microsoft researchers studying a Microsoft tool. Verified. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795
- US, 25 Mar 2026 (Atlanta Fed working paper 2026-4 with Richmond Fed and Duke, nearly 750 executives): the authors name a productivity paradox, perceived AI productivity gains exceed measured gains, likely because revenue effects lag. Verified. https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/working-papers/2026/03/25/04-artificial-intelligence-productivity-and-the-workforce-evidence-from-corporate-executives
- US, Mar 2026 (same CFO survey): executives reported 1.8 percent AI driven labour productivity growth for 2025 and expect 3 percent for 2026. The implied measure built from their own revenue and headcount answers is about 0.6 percent for 2025 and about 1.8 percent expected for 2026. Reported is about three times implied for 2025. Plausible (figures from the Richmond Fed commentary as surfaced in search, page not opened). https://www.richmondfed.org/research/national_economy/cfo_survey/research_and_commentary/2026/20260325_research_commentary
- US, Mar 2026 (ActivTrak, vendor, 11 Mar 2026): logged activity for 163,638 employees at 1,111 organisations, 443 million hours, Jan 2023 to Dec 2025. Productive hours up 5 percent (19 minutes) to 6h36m a day, workday down 2 percent from 8h53m to 8h44m. AI users lost 9 percent of daily focus time (23 minutes), non users unchanged. Verified (vendor data, ActivTrak sells workforce analytics). https://activtrak.com/news/state-of-the-workplace-ai-accelerating-work
- US, Mar 2026 (ActivTrak, vendor): in a subset of 376 companies and 10,584 users, time spent in every measured work category rose between 27 and 346 percent in the 180 days after AI adoption versus the 180 before. This is logged time, not a survey, but it does not isolate the effect of AI from other change. Verified (vendor). https://activtrak.com/news/state-of-the-workplace-ai-accelerating-work
- China, Jul 2026 revision (Alibaba after sales chat, randomised field experiment, arXiv 2603.29888): generative AI drafts raised service speed and customer ratings on average, had no significant effect on the objective quality measure (customer retrials), and lowered both quality measures for top performers. No chats per hour figure in the abstract. Verified (abstract only). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29888
- US, 2025 (Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng, Salz, Management Science): three randomised trials of GitHub Copilot at Microsoft, Accenture and a Fortune 100 firm, 4,867 developers, 26.08 percent more completed tasks (standard error 10.3 percent), gains concentrated in junior developers. Vendor note: Microsoft authors, GitHub tool. Plausible (abstract quoted in search, paper not opened). https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535
- US, May 2026 (BLS notice, last modified 8 May 2026): BLS attributes no share of productivity growth to AI and gives no method for doing so. It points to a May 2026 Monthly Labor Review article on software investment as the related work. Verified. https://www.bls.gov/productivity/notices/2026/productivity-and-artificial-intelligence.htm
- US, 2026: BLS research pages say AI enters official productivity statistics only through the software capital measure, and that more exposed industries show faster labour productivity growth and rising capital intensity (a correlation, not an attribution). Plausible (search summary of the BLS research page, not opened). https://www.bls.gov/productivity/articles-and-research/ai-and-productivity/home.htm
- US, Dec 2025 to May 2026 (Census BTOS): 17 to 20 percent of firms use AI in any business function, 20 to 23 percent expect to within six months. The question wording changed on 17 Nov 2025 so the series is not comparable with 2024. BTOS measures use, not output per hour. Plausible (Census story seen in search, not opened). https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html
- US, 2024 to 2025 (Acemoglu, NBER w32487, published Economic Policy 2025): upper bound of 0.66 percent total factor productivity gain over ten years, falling below 0.53 percent if early task evidence overstates harder tasks. No revision date on the NBER page. Verified. https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487
- US, 2025 (Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, QJE vol 140 issue 2, pages 889 to 942): published version covers 5,172 agents (earlier drafts said 5,179). No 2026 extension found. Verified (publication record). https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:140:y:2025:i:2:p:889-942.
- Overstatement ratios that can be read directly from paired numbers: METR 2025 felt 20 percent faster versus measured 19 percent slower (40 point gap, METR figure). CFO survey 2025 reported 1.8 percent versus implied 0.6 percent (about 3 to 1). Denmark self reported about 3 percent versus pay and hours effects bounded under 2 percent and pass through of 3 to 7 percent. UK GDS 26 minutes self reported versus DWP 19 minutes with an adjusted design (about 1.4 to 1). Verified where the inputs above are Verified, otherwise Plausible. https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/

## Who is working on it

- GenAI Adoption Tracker. Alexander Bick (St Louis Fed), Adam Blandin (Vanderbilt), David Deming (Harvard Project on Workforce). Quarterly since 2024, latest wave Aug 2026, FRED updated 20 Oct 2026. Measures self reported use, hours involving AI and hours saved, with occupation splits. Latest finding: 2.1 percent of all US work hours saved, flat between 2.0 and 2.2 percent for four quarters. https://www.genaiadoptiontracker.com/ and https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RPSGENAITSALL
- Still Waters, Rapid Currents. Anders Humlum (Chicago) and Emilie Vestergaard (Copenhagen). NBER w33777 revised Mar 2026, Brookings write up 6 Oct 2026. Measures self reported adoption and savings against Danish pay and hours registers for about 3 million workers. Latest finding: no pay or hours effect above 2 percent two years on, 85 percent of users redirect saved time to other tasks, new AI tasks appear for 4 in 10 adopters. https://www.brookings.edu/articles/still-waters-rapid-currents-early-labor-market-transformation-under-generative-ai/
- METR developer productivity programme. METR. RCT update 24 Feb 2026, self report survey 11 May 2026, task substitution note 8 May 2026. Measures logged task time with and without AI against developer forecasts and beliefs. Latest finding: 4 to 18 percent faster in late 2025 with intervals spanning zero, self reported speed 3 times, redesign under way with developer level randomisation, fixed task experiments and time use studies. No redesigned result published as of 2 Nov 2026. https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ and https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/
- CFO Survey AI module. Atlanta Fed, Richmond Fed and Duke Fuqua (Baslandze, Edwards, Graham, McClure, Meyer, Sparks, Waddell, Weitz). Working paper 25 Mar 2026, also NBER w34984. Measures executive reported AI productivity against an implied figure from revenue and headcount. Latest finding: reported 1.8 percent versus implied 0.6 percent for 2025, both expected to rise in 2026, authors call the gap a productivity paradox. https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/working-papers/2026/03/25/04-artificial-intelligence-productivity-and-the-workforce-evidence-from-corporate-executives
- Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI. Eleanor Dillon, Sonia Jaffe, Nicole Immorlica (Microsoft Research), Christopher Stanton (Harvard). NBER w33795 revised Nov 2025. Randomised Copilot access at 66 firms, 7,137 workers, six months. Measures time on email, meetings and documents and task composition. Latest finding: about two hours a week off email for users, no change in what tasks workers do. Vendor funded tool and authors. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795
- Alibaba customer service field experiment. Ni, Wang, Feng, Lu, Wang, Zhou. arXiv v2 31 Jul 2026. Randomised AI drafting in after sales chat. Measures speed, customer ratings and customer retrials by agent skill. Latest finding: faster and better rated on average, no gain in objective quality, top agents got worse. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29888
- BLS AI and productivity research. US Bureau of Labor Statistics. Notice 8 May 2026 and Monthly Labor Review May 2026. Measures software investment and industry exposure against official productivity. Latest finding: no attribution of productivity growth to AI, exposure correlates with faster industry productivity. https://www.bls.gov/productivity/notices/2026/productivity-and-artificial-intelligence.htm
- Census BTOS AI supplement. US Census Bureau. Second AI supplement from 17 Nov 2025, story 26 May 2026. Measures firm AI use by function and size. Latest finding: 17 to 20 percent of firms use AI, use is 50 to 70 percent in very large information, professional services and finance firms. No productivity question. https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html
- ActivTrak State of the Workplace (vendor). ActivTrak. 11 Mar 2026, annual. Measures logged application activity, workday length, focus time and time in AI tools. Latest finding: productive hours up 5 percent, workday down 2 percent, focus time down 9 percent for AI users. https://activtrak.com/news/state-of-the-workplace-ai-accelerating-work

## What nobody has measured

- A study that logs the same workers' time with and without AI and also asks them how much they saved, then compares the two numbers person by person. Not found. Search run: "self-reported AI time savings versus measured logged time same workers study". Closest are METR (16 then 57 developers, measured versus believed), Denmark (survey versus pay records, not time logs) and the CFO survey (reported versus implied from revenue). None pairs a worker's own time log with that worker's own estimate.
- A Bick, Blandin, Deming wave that links the 2.1 percent self reported saving to output, pay or hours. Not found. Search run: "Bick Blandin Deming generative AI time saved 2026 update". The tracker to Aug 2026 and FRED to Q3 2026 remain self report only.
- A Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond 2026 extension or second firm. Not found. Search run: "Brynjolfsson Li Raymond generative AI at work 2026 follow-up customer support". Only the QJE 2025 publication record appeared.
- An Acemoglu revision of the 0.66 percent ten year bound. Not found. Search run: "Acemoglu simple macroeconomics of AI 2026 revised estimate TFP". NBER page shows no revision.
- Any official BLS or Federal Reserve Board attribution of a share of 2025 or 2026 US productivity growth to AI. Not found. Search run: "BLS labor productivity 2026 AI attribution Fed research". The BLS notice points to research only. Secondary sites that claim a BLS AI attribution were not confirmed.
- A Census BTOS or BEA measure of output per hour for AI using firms. Not found. Search run: "Census Bureau BTOS AI use productivity 2026". BEA working paper 2026-16 "AI Expectations and Outcomes" was downloaded but its text could not be extracted, so its findings stay unread.
- A field study of agents (tools that act, not chat or drafting assistants) that measures time saved against output. Not found. Search run: "randomized field experiment generative AI workers 2026 firm productivity". All 2026 RCTs located study chat assistants, drafting aids or code completion.
- A METR redesigned RCT result with a tight interval for 2026 tools. Not found. METR says the Feb 2026 data is unreliable and the new design has not reported. Search run: "METR 2026 developer productivity study AI follow up".
- Any measured fall in hours worked tied to AI use at worker level. Not found. Denmark records show no change within 2 percent. ActivTrak shows a 9 minute shorter workday across all staff but does not tie it to AI use. Search run as above.
- The IMF paper on productivity measurement limits named in earlier notes. Not found. Search run: "IMF working paper measuring AI productivity gains national accounts limits". IMF papers located are model projections (Europe, about 1 percent over five years) or patent and usage proxies, not a measurement limits paper. The nearest measurement limits paper is BEA 2025-1 "Concepts and Challenges of Measuring Production of Artificial Intelligence in the US Economy". Plausible (not opened). https://www.bea.gov/index.php/research/papers/2025/concepts-and-challenges-measuring-production-artificial-intelligence-us
- Methods behind the US GSA and Singapore government savings figures. Not re-searched in this pass, left to the policy note.

## One line for a manager

Workers say AI saves them 2 to 3 percent of their hours and executives say 2 to 3 percent productivity, but pay records, hours records and revenue show roughly a third of that or nothing yet, so treat any self reported saving as two to three times the measured one until you have logged data.

# How much of a time saving is lost to rebound, and does anyone have a measured share for time the way energy economics has its 10 to 60 percent?

Researcher note, written 2026-11-02. Scope: the share of saved time that refills with new work, higher standards or new tasks. This note adds to time-study/research/15-time-rebound.md and the rebound rows in 30-policy-report.md and does not repeat the cases already held there (travel time budget, Duranton and Turner, Microsoft 252 percent, housework hours, METR).

Tags. Verified means the page or paper was opened in this pass and the figure read there. Plausible means the figure comes from a search summary or secondary coverage and the primary page was not opened or was blocked. Projection means a model or forecast, not a measurement. Not found means the search ran and nothing turned up, so the item must not be stated as a fact. Vendor figures name the vendor.

## What is known

Direct measures of where saved time goes

- Denmark, 2024 survey round, 25,000 workers at 7,000 workplaces in 11 AI exposed occupations: users of AI chatbots report savings of about 3 percent of their work hours. Verified. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Denmark, 2024 to 2026: 85 percent of chatbot users report putting the saved time into other job tasks, "fewer than 10% report taking additional breaks or leisure", and about 30 percent spend more time on the very tasks they saved time on. This is a share of users, not a share of minutes. Verified (Humlum and Vestergaard, NBER 33777, revised March 2026). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Denmark, 2024: AI chatbots created new workloads for 12 percent of users even where employers ran no initiative, 8 percentage points of that as entirely new tasks (AI oversight, drafting, integration), rising to about 17 to 18 percent of users where employers encourage use and train. Verified. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Denmark, 2022 to 2025 register data: effects of chatbots on earnings and hours are small enough to rule out changes above 2 percent two years after ChatGPT launched. Verified (abstract). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- South Korea, 2026: a representative survey of Korean workers finds 51.8 percent use generative AI for work and "GenAI reduces working time by 3.8%", with the gain captured mainly as on the job leisure rather than more output. Verified (abstract, Suh and Oh, arXiv 13 February 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12695
- South Korea, 2026: the same paper is reported to find a 1.3 percentage point rise in the share of time spent on on the job leisure and that one task accounts for about 70 percent of a user's saving. Plausible (search summary, full text not opened). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12695
- Conflict to carry forward: Denmark says the saving goes back into work, Korea says it goes into slack on the job. Both are self reports from 2024 to 2026 and neither is a minute count.

Vendor and consultancy surveys on where saved time goes (self reported)

- Workday, global, fielded November 2025 by Hanover Research, 3,200 full time AI users at firms with 100 million dollars or more in revenue: "Nearly 40% of AI time savings are lost to rework", 85 percent save 1 to 7 hours a week, 32 percent of organisations respond by raising workloads, 39 percent reinvest in technology and 30 percent in employee development, and only 14 percent of employees consistently see a clear net gain. Verified, vendor (Workday, 14 January 2026). https://en-sg.newsroom.workday.com/2026-01-14-New-Workday-Research-Companies-Are-Leaving-AI-Gains-on-the-Table
- BCG, global, 2026: 42 percent of employees say AI saves them 8 hours a week and more than half say they are not using that time for more strategic work. Sample and countries not stated on the page. Verified, consultancy (BCG, 6 July 2026). https://www.bcg.com/news/6july2026-ai-saving-time-workers-strategically
- UK government, 30 September to 31 December 2024, about 20,000 civil servants on Microsoft 365 Copilot: 26 minutes a day saved by self report, and the report states "it was not possible to identify how time saved was spent". Plausible (The Register, 3 June 2025, quoting the GDS report, PDF not opened). https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/03/uk_government_study_ai_time_savings and https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/683db42bd23a62e5d32680d0/M365_Copilot_Experiment_Findings_Report.pdf

Measured hours and workday shape (telemetry and time diaries)

- US and global, Microsoft 365 telemetry to 15 February 2025 (EU and education tenants excluded): meetings starting after 8 pm up 16 percent year on year, 58 messages per user outside core hours, up 15 percent, 29 percent of active workers back in their inbox by 10 pm, about 275 interruptions a day, 117 emails and 153 Teams messages per person per weekday, and about 20 percent of weekend workers in email before noon on Saturday and Sunday. Verified, vendor (Microsoft Work Trend Index special report, 17 June 2025). https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday
- US centred, January 2023 to December 2025, ActivTrak telemetry on 163,638 employees at 1,111 organisations (443 million hours), with a before and after window of 180 days each side of AI adoption for 376 companies and 10,584 users: AI users' daily focus time fell 9 percent (about 23 minutes) while non users showed no change, the average workday shrank 2 percent from 8 h 53 m to 8 h 44 m, collaboration time rose 34 percent to 52 minutes a day, and 80 percent of employees now use AI tools. Verified, vendor (ActivTrak Productivity Lab, 11 March 2026). https://activtrak.com/news/state-of-the-workplace-ai-accelerating-work
- US centred, 2025 to 2026: coverage of ActivTrak reports says AI users' workdays run about 8 minutes longer than non users'. Plausible, vendor figure in secondary coverage, and the coverage does not agree on which report year it belongs to. https://www.techradar.com/pro/to-ai-or-not-to-ai-how-ai-impacts-your-workforces-productivity
- US, time diary data 2004 to 2023: higher occupational exposure to AI is "associated with longer work hours and reduced leisure time". Verified (abstract, Jiang, Park, Xiao and Zhang, NBER 33536, February 2025). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33536
- US, 2004 to 2023: the same study is summarised as about 2.2 extra hours a week when moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of AI exposure, a correlation not a causal estimate. Plausible (CEPR VoxEU column, page blocked, figure from search summary). https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/ais-power-grows-so-does-our-workday
- US, Q3 2024 to Q2 2026, Real Time Population Survey (Bick, Blandin and Deming): the share of all work hours saved by generative AI rose from 1.6 percent to 2.2 percent, self reported, with nothing on where the time went. Verified (St. Louis Fed FRED blog, 27 August 2026). https://fred.stlouisfed.org/blog/2026/08/does-generative-ai-save-time-at-work/
- US, April to December 2025, one tech firm of about 200 staff: an 8 month field study finds AI users work faster, take on a wider range of tasks and often work longer by choice, with AI use spilling into breaks and evenings. No hours number is given and the study is described as ongoing. Verified for title, authors and date (Ranganathan and Ye, Berkeley Haas, HBR 9 February 2026), Plausible for the design details, which come from heise coverage. https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it and https://www.heise.de/en/news/Study-AI-does-not-make-work-easier-but-more-intense-11171288.html

Updates to the two classic full rebound cases

- England, 2024: people spent 362 hours a year travelling, up from 353 in 2023 and below 370 in 2019, about an hour a day, with 922 trips and 6,082 miles per person. The travel time budget still holds. Verified (National Travel Survey 2024, published 27 August 2025). https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024/nts-2024-introduction-and-main-findings
- US, January 2025: a UC Davis review of the induced travel literature finds the long run elasticity of vehicle miles to lane miles centres around 1.0 within 3 to 10 years, and about 0.3 to 0.8 in the first three years. This is the only field where time rebound has a settled number, and the number is 100 percent. Verified (Handy and Volker, Induced Travel Primer). https://escholarship.org/content/qt0kj840w2/qt0kj840w2_noSplash_98a302e4a4df35efd951177da0374a77.pdf
- US, California, October 2025: Caltrans Task 4491 is re examining those elasticities (truck traffic, non interstate roads, bottleneck projects) with results due November 2026. Plausible (research note summary, PDF not opened). https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/research-innovation-system-information/documents/research-notes/task4491-rns-10-2025-a11y.pdf

Energy economics on the time side

- Japan, 2018: Mizobuchi and Yamagami measure a time rebound effect in household energy use, where dishwashers, dryers and delivery services free time that is spent on energy using activities. Extra electricity is about 1.4 percent of daily use at most and "its impact is very small". This measures energy from saved time, not time refilled. Verified (abstract, FAERE working paper 2018.20). https://ideas.repec.org/p/fae/wpaper/2018.20.html
- Germany, 2025: a Sustainability paper models rebound from AI and robots and treats the free time they create as the major part of the rebound in its worked example, proposing a CO2 budget to cap it. Projection, Plausible (page blocked twice, search summary only). https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/5/1988

## Who is working on it

- Anders Humlum (Chicago Booth) and Emilie Vestergaard (University of Copenhagen). NBER working paper 33777, first issued May 2025, revised March 2026, built on survey rounds in 2023 and late 2024 linked to Danish registers. Measures chatbot adoption, self reported time saved, new tasks, and register earnings and hours. Latest finding: about 3 percent of hours saved, 85 percent of users put it back into work, under 10 percent into rest, no earnings or hours effect above 2 percent. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Donghyun Suh and Samil Oh. arXiv 2602.12695, 13 February 2026, representative survey of Korean workers. Measures working time reduction from generative AI and how the saving is split between output, on the job leisure and more fulfilling work. Latest finding: 3.8 percent less working time, captured mainly as on the job leisure. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12695
- Alexander Bick, Adam Blandin and David Deming, with the St. Louis Fed Generative AI Adoption Tracker. Quarterly Real Time Population Survey since Q3 2024. Measures the share of all US work hours saved by generative AI. Latest finding, 27 August 2026: 2.2 percent of hours in Q2 2026, up from 1.6 percent. Does not ask where the time goes. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/blog/2026/08/does-generative-ai-save-time-at-work/
- Wei Jiang, Junyoung Park, Rachel Xiao and Shen Zhang. NBER working paper 33536, February 2025. Uses US time diaries 2004 to 2023 to relate AI exposure to hours and leisure. Latest finding: more exposure goes with longer hours and less leisure. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33536
- Aruna Ranganathan and Xingqi Maggie Ye, Berkeley Haas. Ongoing field study at one US tech firm, April to December 2025 so far, reported in HBR on 9 February 2026. Observes task expansion, blurred boundaries and multitasking after AI rollout. No numeric split yet. https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it
- ActivTrak Productivity Lab (vendor). Annual State of the Workplace, latest 11 March 2026, telemetry before and after AI adoption on 163,638 employees. Measures workday length, focus time and collaboration time. Latest finding: AI users lose 23 minutes a day of focus time while the average workday is 2 percent shorter. https://activtrak.com/news/state-of-the-workplace-ai-accelerating-work
- Microsoft WorkLab Work Trend Index (vendor). Special report on the infinite workday, 17 June 2025, Microsoft 365 telemetry. Measures after hours meetings, messages and interruptions. Latest finding: after 8 pm meetings up 16 percent, 58 after hours messages per person. The 2026 annual report exists but its meeting hours figure was not reached on a Microsoft page in this pass. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday
- Workday with Hanover Research (vendor). Survey of 3,200 AI users, November 2025, released 14 January 2026. Measures hours saved, rework and how employers redeploy the gain. Latest finding: nearly 40 percent of the saving lost to rework. https://en-sg.newsroom.workday.com/2026-01-14-New-Workday-Research-Companies-Are-Leaving-AI-Gains-on-the-Table
- Susan Handy and Jamey Volker, UC Davis National Center for Sustainable Transportation, with Caltrans Task 4491. Induced travel elasticities, primer January 2025, Caltrans update due November 2026. Latest finding: long run elasticity about 1.0. https://escholarship.org/content/qt0kj840w2/qt0kj840w2_noSplash_98a302e4a4df35efd951177da0374a77.pdf
- UK Department for Transport, National Travel Survey, annual. Latest finding, 27 August 2025: 362 hours of travel per person in 2024. https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024/nts-2024-introduction-and-main-findings
- UK Government Digital Service. Microsoft 365 Copilot experiment, Q4 2024, report June 2025. Measured minutes saved per day but could not say where they went. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/683db42bd23a62e5d32680d0/M365_Copilot_Experiment_Findings_Report.pdf

## What nobody has measured

- A share of saved minutes that refills, counted by time diary or telemetry before and after a tool, rather than by asking workers how they used the time. Not found. Searches run: "time rebound" effect time savings share refilled 2025, and workers AI time saved what do they do with it survey 2025 percent more work. The Danish 85 percent and Workday 40 percent are the closest and both are self reports.
- Any OECD, European Commission JRC, UK Government Office for Science or Japan Cabinet Office report that uses the term time rebound or tracks where saved time goes. Not found. Searches run: OECD OR JRC OR "Government Office for Science" generative AI working time saved hours where time goes report 2025, and OECD Employment Outlook 2025 artificial intelligence hours worked time savings reinvested tasks. The nearest official document is the UK GDS Copilot report, which says it could not identify how saved time was spent. Japan Cabinet Office material was not reached in English.
- A firm level study that reports output, hours and the split of the saving for one time saving tool. Not found. ActivTrak gives hours and focus time without output, the Berkeley Haas study gives direction without numbers, and the Danish study gives self reported allocation without output. Searches run: work intensification after automation AI 2025 study hours increased workload, and Harvard Business Review Berkeley AI work intensification study 200 employees tech company.
- A time rebound share for any field other than road capacity. Induced travel has its 1.0, nothing else has a number. Not found. Search run: Jevons paradox AI productivity time saved more work 2026 study, which returned commentary and secondary summaries only.
- A 2025 or 2026 update of the travel time budget outside England, for example from the US American Time Use Survey. Not found. Search run: travel time budget Marchetti constant 2025 national travel survey hours per year.
- The Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026 meeting hours figure on a Microsoft page. Not found. Search run: Microsoft Work Trend Index 2026 report meetings hours findings. A third party blog restates 15.4 hours a week in meetings and that figure is unverified.
- A reconciliation of the Danish result (saving goes back into work) and the Korean result (saving goes into on the job leisure). Not found in any source. Both are 2026 self report studies and no third study tests the two against each other.

## One line for a manager

Nobody has a measured share of saved time that refills, but the best count so far (Denmark, 25,000 workers, revised March 2026) says 85 percent of AI users put the saving straight back into work and under 10 percent into rest, and a Workday survey (2026) says about 40 percent of the saving is eaten by rework, so plan for most of any saving to return as more work unless you decide where it goes first.

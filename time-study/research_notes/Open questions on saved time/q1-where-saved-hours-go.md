# Where does a saved hour go, kept as free time, reinvested in paid work or lost to new tasks and standards, who measures this today, and does any study project where saved or free time will go in future?

Researcher note, 2 Nov 2026. Scope is Q1 only. Figures the study already holds (BCG 42 percent and eight hours, Aksoy 40 percent to work and 11 percent to care, NBER 33536 at 3.15 and 3.20 hours, UK GDS 26 minutes, Korea near zero link between time saved and output) are not repeated except where a newer version changes them. Tags follow the study convention: Verified means the primary page or PDF was opened, Plausible means a search summary or trade press was the source, Projection means a forward estimate, Not found means searched and empty.

## What is known

- Denmark, 2024 survey round, about 25,000 workers in 11 exposed occupations: adopters save about 3 percent of work hours. Asked what they do with the saving, 85 percent of chatbot users put it into other job tasks, fewer than 10 percent take more breaks or leisure, and about 30 percent spend more time on the same task they saved time on. Humlum and Vestergaard, NBER 33777, revised March 2026 (new title Still Waters, Rapid Currents). Verified (full PDF read). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Same study: the survey item is worded as an expectation, "If AI chatbots save time on a task, do you expect to", so the 85, 10 and 30 are stated intentions from a tick list, not diary or telemetry measures. Verified (questionnaire in the appendix, item 15). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Same study: about 8 percent of chatbot users took on entirely new tasks (content generation, AI oversight, AI integration), rising to about 17 to 18 percent where employers ran chatbot initiatives. New tasks also reached workers who never used a chatbot. Verified (full PDF read). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Same study, Denmark, register data through December 2024: precise null on earnings and recorded hours at worker and workplace level, ruling out effects larger than 2 percent two years after ChatGPT launched. Verified (abstract and text). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- United States, Q2 2026: 39.2 percent of employed adults used generative AI for work in the past week, up from 28.2 percent in Q3 2024. Hours saved as a share of all US work hours rose from 1.6 percent to 2.2 percent over the same stretch. The post says nothing about where the saved hours go. Bick, Blandin and Deming, Real-Time Population Survey, FRED blog 27 Aug 2026. Verified (page read). https://fred.stlouisfed.org/blog/2026/08/does-generative-ai-save-time-at-work/
- United States, ATUS diaries 2004 to 2023: moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of generative AI exposure went with 3.15 more work hours and 3.20 fewer leisure hours a week after ChatGPT, the loss concentrated in non screen leisure (relaxing, socialising, travel). Latest draft dated 17 Dec 2025, on the AEA January 2026 programme. No 2026 revision found. Jiang, Park, Xiao and Zhang, NBER 33536. Direction Verified (NBER abstract, already in the study), draft date and magnitudes Plausible (AEA programme listing and The Register via search). https://www.nber.org/papers/w33536 and https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/69TEQ7rF
- United States, ATUS 2017 to 2018 against 2023 to 2024, wage and salary workers: a work from home day removes 52 minutes of commuting. In 2023 to 2024 men put the saved time mainly into leisure and sleep, women into household chores (hybrid days) and primary childcare (remote mothers). Sleep rose about 4 percent across all arrangements. Paid hours no longer differ by work location. Pabilonia (US BLS) and Vernon (SUNY Empire State), 31 Dec 2025, AEA 2026 programme. Verified (full PDF read). https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/fnY372QS
- 27 countries, 2021 to 2022 surveys: 72 minutes a day saved by working from home, 40 percent to work, 34 percent to leisure, 11 percent to care. Published as Aksoy, Barrero, Bloom, Davis, Dolls and Zarate, AEA Papers and Proceedings vol 113, 2023, pages 597 to 603. No 2025 or 2026 follow up with new allocation shares found. Plausible (search summary of BFI and C.D. Howe pages, paper not opened this round). https://bfi.uchicago.edu/insight/research-summary/time-savings-when-working-from-home
- United States, 2025 (released 25 Jun 2026): leisure and sports 5.16 hours a day (men 5.58, women 4.76). TV 2.61 hours. Games and leisure computing 37 minutes, up from 25 in 2015. Socialising 35 minutes, down from 41 in 2015, done by 30 percent of people on an average day against 38 percent in 2015. Full time workers 8.1 hours on days worked. ATUS has no item on AI or on saved time. Operations suspended 1 Oct to 12 Nov 2025 for the federal shutdown. US BLS. Verified (release read). https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06252026.htm
- United States, United Kingdom and Australia, June 2026, about 6,000 digital workers: workers estimate AI saves them about 11 hours a week but spend 6.4 hours a week (37 percent of AI related time) feeding context, supervising, fixing and cleaning AI output, which the vendor calls botsitting. 13 percent say AI significantly improved their organisation's performance. Glean Work AI Institute (vendor survey). Plausible (IT Brief 16 Jun 2026 read, primary report not opened). https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-saves-workers-11-hours-but-botsitting-eats-gains
- Global, June 2026, 3,200 business leaders: 85 percent of employees save one to seven hours a week with AI and nearly 40 percent of the saved time is lost at once to rework. Workday (vendor survey of leaders, not of workers). Plausible (The Next Web 3 Jun 2026 read, Workday report not opened). https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-saves-time-companies-waste-the-gain
- 14 markets, 2026, 11,749 workers: 66 percent of workers get no guidance on what to do with the time AI saves. BCG AI at Work 2026 (vendor survey), adds to the 42 percent and eight hours already in the study. Plausible (trade press). https://www.bcg.com/news/6july2026-ai-saving-time-workers-strategically
- United Kingdom: the ONS Time use in the UK page lists wave 9 (2025) and wave 10 (2026) as spreadsheets with no bulletin. No wave 10 free time figure surfaced in search. The latest published figure stays autumn 2023 at 3 hours 39 minutes of free time a day. Not found (2026 figure). https://www.ons.gov.uk/releases/timeuseintheuk
- Forward projection of free time use: none found from a named institution with hours or shares. Frost and Sullivan sells a paywalled Future of Free Time paper to 2030 (not read). Ipsos What the Future: Leisure gives scenarios with no hours. Not found. https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/future/leisure

## Who is working on it

- Humlum and Vestergaard (Chicago Booth and University of Copenhagen), NBER 33777, revised March 2026. Repeated surveys of about 25,000 Danish workers in 11 occupations linked to tax and hours registers. Measures adoption, self reported time saving, stated use of saved time, new tasks, and register earnings and hours. Latest finding: 85 percent reinvest in other work, under 10 percent in breaks or leisure, about 30 percent in more of the same task, and hours and pay unchanged within 2 percent. The only study found that asks the kept, reinvested, lost question directly. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777
- Bick, Blandin and Deming (St. Louis Fed, Vanderbilt, Harvard), Real-Time Population Survey and the FRED Generative AI Adoption Tracker, quarterly, latest Q2 2026 posted 27 Aug 2026. Measures adoption and hours saved as a share of all US work hours (2.2 percent). Does not ask where the hours go. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RPSGENAITSALL
- Jiang, Park, Xiao and Zhang (Emory and co-authors), AI and the Extended Workday, NBER 33536, draft 17 Dec 2025, AEA 2026. Uses ATUS diaries to measure work and leisure hours by occupational AI exposure. Latest finding: more work, less non screen leisure. The only diary based evidence on the free time column, and it runs through 2023 only. https://www.nber.org/papers/w33536
- Pabilonia and Vernon (US BLS and SUNY Empire State), How did the remote work revolution change our work and leisure time, 31 Dec 2025, AEA 2026. Uses ATUS diaries 2017 to 2018 and 2023 to 2024 linked to CPS work from home intensity. The best current template for following a saved hour (the commute) into sleep, leisure, chores and childcare by sex and parenthood. Not about AI. https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/fnY372QS
- Aksoy, Barrero, Bloom, Davis, Dolls and Zarate (Stanford, ITAM, EBRD, ifo and co-authors), Global Survey of Working Arrangements. The 2023 paper is the origin of the 40, 34, 11 split. No 2025 or 2026 re-run of the allocation question found. https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp10240.pdf
- US BLS American Time Use Survey, annual, latest 25 Jun 2026 for 2025. Measures total time by activity. Has no AI or saved time item. https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06252026.htm
- Vendors: BCG AI at Work (6 Jul 2026), Glean Work AI Institute (Jun 2026), Workday (Jun 2026). Each measures self reported hours saved and one form of leakage (no guidance, botsitting, rework). None measures leisure, pay or hours worked.

## What nobody has measured

- Not found: any study that follows the same workers' saved AI hours with a time diary or telemetry and splits them into free time, more paid work, and new tasks or higher standards. Searches run: "firm AI rollout time saved where did it go study 2026 workers reinvested hours", "Humlum Vestergaard 2025 generative AI Denmark time savings what workers did with saved time", "Bick Blandin Deming 2026 generative AI adoption survey time saved hours". The nearest is Humlum's stated intention item (85, 10, 30) and Glean's 6.4 hours of botsitting (vendor).
- Not found: a measured lost to standards share, meaning time absorbed by polishing, extra review or higher expectations for the same output. Humlum's about 30 percent spending more time on the same tasks and Workday's nearly 40 percent lost to rework are the only numbers and both are self reported. Searches as above.
- Not found: any 2025 or 2026 re-run of the commute allocation question (work, leisure, care shares) by Aksoy, Barrero, Bloom and co-authors. Search run: "WFH Research SWAA 2025 commute time savings allocation work leisure caregiving update". Pabilonia and Vernon (Dec 2025) give direction by sex but no percentage split.
- Not found: an ATUS or ONS item that attributes any minute of the day to AI or to time saved. ATUS June 2026 release read, ONS release page checked. Searches run: "American Time Use Survey 2025 results June 2026 leisure hours", "ONS time use in the UK 2026 wave 10 free time minutes per day".
- Not found: a forward projection by a named institution of how free time will be used in 2030 or later, with hours or shares. Search run: "forecast leisure time 2035 projection how people will spend free time AI institute report". Only a paywalled Frost and Sullivan paper (2030) and Ipsos scenarios surfaced. This repeats the empty result already logged in research/32-debate-map-gaps.md.
- Not found: a firm level evaluation after an AI rollout that reports where saved hours went in hours worked, overtime, leave or pay. Vendor surveys (BCG, Glean, Workday) stop at not redirected or lost to rework. Search run: "firm AI rollout time saved where did it go study 2026 workers reinvested hours".
- Not found: diary evidence on AI and leisure after 2023. NBER 33536 ends with 2023 diaries and no version found has run the 2024 or 2025 ATUS years through that design.

## One line for a manager

Workers say a saved AI hour goes back into other work and almost never into free time, pay and hours have not moved, and no one has yet followed that hour home with a diary or published a forecast of where it goes next.

# 17. Horizons by scenario: industry, business, behaviour

Lens: time rebound, AI as the main new driver. All cells are Projection. Hours fixed means saving written into shorter hours. Full rebound means saving spent on more work and output. Energy-limited means AI spread capped by power.

## 1 year (2027)

Confidence: High. Signal: Directive share in Anthropic Economic Index: under 40% slow, over 55% fast

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Office work uses AI for email and drafts, jobs steady | Seat pricing holds, saved minutes logged as earlier finish | Hours flat, a few four day pilots, little felt change |
| Base | Software and writing tasks shift first, junior hiring slows | Hybrid seat plus usage pricing, saved time fills with tasks | Hours flat, more tasks per hour, rushed feeling unchanged |
| Full rebound | Firms cut junior hiring sharply and pilot agent teams | Saved time resold as more output, volume targets rise | More meetings and messages, felt rush rises like after Teams |
| Energy-limited | Big cloud regions get AI first, others wait for power | Usage priced by compute, rationed tiers for heavy users | Same as base for most, slower rollout outside tech hubs |

## 3 years (2029)

Confidence: Medium high. Signal: Graduate unemployment gap vs all workers widening over 2 points means fast

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Support and back office automate, staff move to shorter weeks | Outcome pricing starts, saving booked as paid time off | Full time hours 38 to 40, more async, less rush |
| Base | Support and back office automate, entry roles thin | 2% to 5% of firms on four days, outcome pricing spreads | Free time flat in total, more of it on screens |
| Full rebound | Entry roles shrink, agent labour replaces junior desks | Firms bank saving as growth, headcount flat, output up | Work expands to fill hours, focus spans keep shrinking |
| Energy-limited | Data centre and grid trades boom, AI work clusters near power | Compute cost sets margins, energy deals become strategy | Gains uneven by region, rush stays where power is short |

## 5 years (2031)

Confidence: Medium. Signal: OECD annual hours per worker: a fall over 3% from 2026 means fast

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Exposed white collar sectors trade output growth for shorter hours | Four day week used as a hiring perk | Hours down in exposed sectors, writing and recall skills fade |
| Base | Agent labour a budget line, BPO and call centres shrink | Agent costs sit beside payroll, pricing by outcome | Hours down 2% to 4%, clear deskilling in writing |
| Full rebound | Exposed sectors run more output, not fewer hours | Saved hours sold as speed, market share races | Hours roughly flat, felt time poverty stays near 80% |
| Energy-limited | Data centres near 945 TWh, power sets where AI spreads | Firms buy power before models, compute rationed by price | Hour cuts arrive years later in power tight regions |

## 10 years (2036)

Confidence: Medium low. Signal: Labour share of national income falling below its historic range

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Four days common in white collar work, jobs mostly kept | Productivity paid out as time, prices fall slowly | Leisure up 2 to 4 hrs a week, rush eases |
| Base | Routine office, support and outsourcing work largely agent run | Agent firms with small staff, outcome pricing the norm | 34 to 36 hrs a week, offloading accepted like calculators |
| Full rebound | New AI made services absorb freed labour, output soars | Growth captures saving, labour share under pressure | Hours barely fall, life fills with new tasks and media |
| Energy-limited | Energy and grid sectors set the pace of AI jobs | Compute access priced like a utility, power rich regions win | Hour gains split by region, gap with power poor areas |

## 15 years (2041)

Confidence: Low. Signal: Share of under 35s who say paid work is their main identity

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Care, craft and community work grow as paid hours shrink | Firms compete on time off, not pay | About 32 hrs, portfolio lives of paid, care and craft |
| Base | Most knowledge work agent assisted, care jobs grow | Small firms run large agent fleets, pricing by result | Leisure about a third higher than 2026, gaps by gender remain |
| Full rebound | Entire service sectors rebuilt around agents, few desk jobs | Saving spent on new products, income support debated | Hours stay high for many, rush and burnout persist |
| Energy-limited | Power build out decides which countries automate first | Cheap power regions host AI, others license services | Shorter hours mostly in power rich countries |

## 25 years (2051)

Confidence: Very low. Signal: Median paid hours per adult across OECD under 15 a week means fast

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Paid work shared across more people at fewer hours | Productivity dividend paid as time, not extra goods | About 28 to 30 hrs, deliberate skill training common |
| Base | Human work concentrates in care, judgement and trust roles | Agent run firms, people paid for taste and accountability | Large leisure gain, identity shifts to craft and community |
| Full rebound | Paid work a minority activity, output many times larger | Most value made by agents, ownership decides income | Free time full of status games, felt rush may stay |
| Energy-limited | Energy supply caps AI share of work in many regions | Margins tied to power cost, energy rich firms dominate | Hour cuts lag a decade where power stays short |

## 50 years (2076)

Confidence: Speculative. Signal: Whether hours per worker fell faster after AI than after electricity

| Scenario | Industry | Business | Human behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours fixed | Work week set by law and custom, AI fills the gap | Firms sell fewer hours of human work at higher value | Keynes partly right a century late, much more free time |
| Base | Most production automated, humans in care, craft and oversight | Pricing by outcome, human work a premium label | 20 to 25 hrs, post work identity mainstream |
| Full rebound | Work optional for most, new industries invent new demand | Saving fully re-spent on output, consumption far higher | Time feels as scarce as ever despite huge leisure |
| Energy-limited | Power supply, not models, sets the limit on automation | Energy cost is the core input price for all firms | Free time gains track energy access across countries |

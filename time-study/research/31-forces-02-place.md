# Place force on time use: commuting, remote and hybrid work, housing costs, autonomous vehicles and city form

Research date: 1 November 2026. Scope: US, UK, EU (France, Germany, Nordics), Japan, Korea, China. Every number carries year, country, a status label and a source line. Status labels: Verified means I opened the source and read the number there. Plausible means a secondary source or a search snippet of the primary that I could not open in full. Not found means no source gave the number. Future numbers are labelled Projection or Scenario.

Reading note on units. Time use surveys report minutes or hours per day averaged over all people, including those who did not do the activity that day. Travel surveys report hours per person per year. I convert to hours per week where useful and say so. 1 hour per day equals 7 hours per week.

## Key question 1: commute and total daily travel time by decade

### Takeaway
Total travel time per person sits close to one hour a day in every rich country with a long series (UK about 60 minutes in 2024, US about 59 to 62 minutes in 2024 and 2025, France 62 minutes in 2019, Norway 71 minutes in 2018 to 2019, Germany 84 minutes in 2023), and it has moved by only a few minutes a decade. The pandemic cut US work travel from 17 to 13 minutes a day per adult, and the UK lost 8 hours a year of travel between 2019 and 2024, but one way commute length for those who still commute is back near its 2019 peak.

### Inferences
- Across the US, UK, France, Germany and Norway the total travel budget stays between 59 and 84 minutes a day and shifts by single digit minutes per decade, so the Place force acts mostly on what travel time is used for, not on how much of it there is.
- The US pandemic shift took about 4 minutes a day of work travel out of the average adult's day (0.28 to 0.22 hours), roughly 25 minutes a week per adult. Over the same years household activities rose from 1.78 to 1.99 hours a day, about 1.5 hours a week per adult, which is a bigger move than the travel saving alone explains.
- UK commuting trips per person fell about a fifth from 2019 to 2024 while total travel hours fell only 2 percent, which fits the travel budget idea: time freed from commuting was spent on other trips.

## Key question 2: remote and hybrid work share and its measured effect on travel and paid hours

### Takeaway
US work from home has been flat at about a quarter of paid days since mid 2022 (26 percent in July 2026), UK hybrid working sits at 28 percent of workers, and the EU share who usually work from home is about 9 percent. The best evidence says working from home saves about 72 minutes a day on commute days, about 2 hours a week per worker in 2021 to 2022 and about 1 hour a week once the pandemic effect faded, with roughly 40 percent of the saved time flowing back into paid work.

### Inferences
- Applying the Aksoy et al. split to the US: 26 percent of paid days at home, about 55 minutes saved per home day, 5 paid days, gives about 72 minutes a week saved per worker, of which about 30 minutes goes back to the job and about 40 minutes to leisure, chores and care. For the whole adult population (about 60 percent employed) that is roughly 25 minutes a week to free time per adult, which matches the fall in work travel in ATUS.
- The remote work effect on paid hours is small and positive, not negative: saved commute time partly becomes extra paid work, so the Place force through remote work nudges paid hours up slightly and free hours up slightly at the same time, funded by travel time.
- Since the share of paid days at home has been flat for four years and employer plans match it, the incremental effect over the next 1 to 5 years is close to zero unless return to office mandates bite or AI tools widen remote feasible jobs.

### Gaps

- The Japanese MLIT 2024 telework share was masked in the Statista snippet and not found elsewhere.
- No study found that measures how saved commute time is spent in 2025 or 2026 specifically. The Aksoy et al. estimates are from 2021 to 2022 surveys.

## Key question 3: housing cost burden and its link to hours worked and multiple jobs

### Takeaway
Housing cost burdens are at record levels in the US (49 to 50 percent of renter households pay over 30 percent of income, 22.6 to 22.7 million households) and high in England (private renters spend 34 percent of income on housing), and US multiple jobholding rose from 5.2 percent of the employed in 2024 to 5.4 percent in 2025 with a record 9.3 million in November 2025. The direction fits the story that housing costs push paid hours up, but no causal study linking rent burden to second jobs was found, and the one causal rent study runs the other way (cheaper rent cuts hours).

Status: Plausible (JCHS site returned 403, figures from NLIHC and press summaries). Source: [NLIHC summary of JCHS 2025](https://nlihc.org/resource/latest-state-nations-housing-report-finds-record-number-housing-cost-burdened-renters) and [JCHS press release copy](https://yieldpro.com/pdf/infographics/2025/0506/Harvard_JCHS_State_of_the_Nations_Housing_2025_Press_Release.pdf)

Home prices up 54 percent since 2020. In 2025 the median existing single family price was nearly five times median household income. Residential mobility 11.2 percent in 2024, a record low. ### Inferences
- The multiple jobholding rise from 5.2 to 5.4 percent of the employed is about 370,000 more people. If a second job adds about 13 hours a week (a common BLS secondary job length, not verified here), that is roughly 4.8 million extra paid hours a week spread over about 165 million employed, or under 2 minutes a week per worker. The direction is up but the strength is weak.
- The causal evidence runs through wealth for owners (higher prices, fewer hours) and through cost for renters (cheaper rent, fewer hours, so dearer rent, more hours). With half of US renters cost burdened and owners sitting on 54 percent price gains since 2020, the two channels partly cancel at the national level.

### Gaps
- No study found that directly estimates the effect of rent burden on multiple jobholding or second job hours. The Bankrate 35 percent is a self reported use of income, not a causal link.
- OECD HC1.2 medians for the US, UK, France, Germany, Japan and Korea could not be read from text and are Not found here.
- Japan and Korea housing cost shares of income were not researched beyond the OECD page.

## Key question 4: autonomous vehicle timelines and their effect on travel time

### Takeaway
Robotaxis are real but tiny: Waymo went from 50,000 paid rides a week in May 2024 to 500,000 a week in March 2026 across 10 US cities with about 3,000 vehicles, against roughly a billion person trips a day in the US. Industry surveys pushed adoption timelines out by one to two years in 2025 to 2026, with private self driving cars not expected in urban pilots before about 2032. Research on value of travel time suggests riding instead of driving cuts the felt cost of travel time by 20 to 30 percent, which planners expect to induce more travel rather than save hours.

### Cited Findings
- US, March 2026: Waymo provides 500,000 paid robotaxi rides a week across 10 cities (Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Austin, Atlanta, Miami, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Orlando), up from 50,000 a week in May 2024. Fleet 3,067 fifth generation robotaxis per NHTSA data from December 2025. Status: Verified (TechCrunch, company reported figures). Source: [TechCrunch, 27 March 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/27/waymo-skyrocketing-ridership-in-one-chart/)
- US, April 2025: more than 250,000 paid rides a week (Alphabet earnings). May 2025: 10 million cumulative paid trips. December 2025: 450,000 a week. Target 1 million a week by end 2026. Status: Plausible. Source: [Entrepreneur, April 2025](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/waymo-reports-robotaxis-are-booked-250000-times-a-week/490644) and [NBC Bay Area, May 2025](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/business/money-report/waymo-says-it-reached-10-million-robotaxi-trips-doubling-in-five-months/3873528/)
- Global, McKinsey Center for Future Mobility third Autonomous Vehicle Industry Leader Survey (white paper dated June 2026 in one summary, survey fielded January 2025 with 91 decision makers in another): widespread adoption delayed by about 1 to 2 years for most use cases against the 2023 survey. Robotaxis and autonomous trucks expected to commercialise before privately owned cars. Level 4 hub to hub trucking expected 2029. Urban pilots for private autonomous cars likely delayed to 2032. 49 percent expect private vehicles to centre on Level 2 plus rather than Level 3. Status: Plausible (secondary summaries, McKinsey page not opened). Scenario. Source: [eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/158514) and [Washinmura summary of McKinsey, 11 June 2026](https://aeo.washinmura.jp/ai/mckinsey-com/en/news/2026-06-11-%E8%87%AA%E5%8B%95%E9%81%8B%E8%BB%A2-%E7%84%A6%E7%82%B9%E3%81%AF-%E5%AE%8C%E5%85%A8%E8%87%AA%E5%8B%95%E5%8C%96%E3%81%AE%E6%9C%9F%E5%BE%85-%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89-%E5%95%86%E7%94%A8%E5%8C%96%E3%81%AE%E5%8B%9D%E3%81%A1%E7%AD%8B-%E3%81%B8)
- Germany, January 2022: McKinsey experts then expected widely available robotaxi offers in 2026. Status: Plausible. Scenario (superseded). Source: [McKinsey Germany press release, 3 January 2022](https://www.mckinsey.com/de/news/presse/2022-01-03-autonomes-fahren)
- McKinsey cost view: a robotaxi ride costs around 8 dollars a mile today, and car ownership could stop being economical for half of people in a major city if robotaxi costs keep falling. Status: Plausible. Scenario. Source: [WardsAuto on McKinsey at CES](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/robotaxi-adas-growth-fueled-by-falling-tech-costs/798830/)
- Finland, driving simulator study (Lehtonen et al., Transportation 2021): about 30 percent saving in perceived value of travel time on routes where automation is available. Status: Plausible (abstract). Source: [Lehtonen et al. 2021](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11116-021-10211-6.pdf)
- US, revealed preference (Jabbari, University of Washington 2022): riding in a car rather than driving lowers value of travel time by about 23 dollars an hour, framed as a driver of induced demand. Status: Plausible. Source: [UW ResearchWorks](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/3564cbe8-c398-4867-adb2-37f53a0fdc1d/full)
- Germany, KIT commuting case study (Szimba and Hartmann, WCTR 2019): travel time savings up to 20 percent with Level 4 vehicles. Status: Plausible. Source: [WCTR 2019 paper](https://www.wctrs-society.com/wp-content/uploads/abstracts/mumbai/WCTR2019_Mumbai_159.pdf)
- US, Taiebat et al. (Applied Energy 2019): combined fuel and time inclusive price elasticity of vehicle miles travelled of minus 0.39, so a lower time cost of travel raises miles driven. Status: Plausible. Source: [arXiv 1902.00382](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1902.00382)
- England and Wales, DfT National Road Traffic Projections 2022: distance driven rises 8 to 54 percent between 2025 and 2060 across scenarios, core scenario about 22 percent. A connected and autonomous vehicle scenario is the upper end and press coverage said self driving cars could nearly double congestion. Status: Plausible (report PDF seen in search, not opened). Projection. Source: [DfT NRTP 2022](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63975bcfd3bf7f3f7d1cf440/national-road-traffic-projections-2022.pdf) and [Highways News](https://highways-news.com/?p=21232)
- Global, ITF Transport Outlook 2023: passenger demand up 79 percent by 2050 under Current Ambition, 65 percent under High Ambition. Urban passenger kilometres up 74 percent 2019 to 2050 under Current Ambition. The 2025 Outlook exists (launched May 2025) but its page returned 403. Status: Plausible. Projection. Source: [ITF Transport Outlook 2023](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/itf-transport-outlook-2023_b6cc9ad5-en.html)

### Inferences
- 500,000 rides a week is about 70,000 a day. US adults make on the order of a billion trips a day (ATUS shows about one hour of travel per person), so robotaxis carry well under 0.01 percent of trips in 2026. Even tenfold growth a year for three years leaves them under 10 percent of trips in the handful of cities served.
- The literature agrees automation lowers the felt cost of time in the vehicle by 20 to 30 percent. Under a stable travel time budget that shows up as longer trips and more sprawl, not fewer travel hours. The gain to free hours comes from being able to work, read or rest while moving, which is a reclassification of the same hour.
- For the 25 and 50 year horizons, the plausible effect is that part of the roughly 7 hours a week of travel becomes usable time. If half of car time became usable and cars are about 60 percent of travel time, that is about 2 hours a week per adult of newly usable time, part of which would be paid work by the same 40 percent rule seen in work from home.

### Gaps
- No published Waymo rider data on what riders do during trips (work, rest, phone) was found. Only ride counts and fleet size.
- ITF Transport Outlook 2025 figures could not be opened (403). The 2023 edition is used instead.
- No peer reviewed estimate of hours of travel time per person under automation for 2036, 2051 or 2076 was found.

## Key question 5: the Marchetti constant and city density

### Takeaway
The one hour a day travel budget still holds at the aggregate level in the latest surveys: England 60 minutes a day in 2024 (59.5 minutes from 362 hours a year) against 61 minutes in 2019, the US about 59 to 62 minutes a day in 2024 and 2025 against 70 in 2019, France 62 minutes, Norway 71, Germany 84 and Korea close to 108. The literature review puts the regularity at about 70 minutes plus or minus 10 across four decades and says its cause is still unknown.

### Cited Findings
- Review of about four decades of travel time budget studies: a daily regularity of about 70 minutes is consistently reported, but the cause of the aggregate regularity is still not known and cross comparison is not possible because studies differ in data and methods. Status: Plausible (abstract only, article paywalled). Source: [Ahmed and Stopher, Seventy Minutes Plus or Minus 10, Transport Reviews 34(5), 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/transr/v34y2014i5p607-625.html)
- Marchetti 1994: average daily travel time stays roughly fixed at about one hour as transport changes, a finding Marchetti attributed to Yacov Zahavi. Status: Plausible. Source: [Wikipedia, Marchetti's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchetti%27s_constant)
- Portugal, Ivory Coast and Boston mobile phone data, 2012 to 2013: morning commute tolerance about 70 minutes in Portugal regardless of distance, just under 80 minutes in Ivory Coast, described as a localized form of Marchetti's idea. Status: Plausible. Source: [MIT Technology Review, 21 November 2013](https://www.technologyreview.com/s/521911/universal-law-of-commuting-discovered-in-african-european-and-us-mobile-phone-data/)
- UK, David Metz: British survey data show stable travel time, so infrastructure that saves time is spent on longer distance. Status: Plausible. Source: [Wikipedia, Marchetti's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchetti%27s_constant)
- Counter evidence: an OECD focused analysis argues daily travel times have steadily grown in most OECD countries and that fixed budgets should not be assumed in planning. Status: Plausible (cited in a Dutch dissertation). Source: [Van 't Hoen dissertation, TRAIL 2023](https://rstrail.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/N-Hoen-Maarten-t.pdf)
- Latest data points, repeated from question 1: England 362 hours a year in 2024 (Verified), US 0.99 hours a day in 2025 (Verified rows, derived sum), France 1 hour 02 in 2019 (Plausible), Germany 84 minutes in 2023 (Plausible), Norway 71 minutes in 2018 to 2019 (Plausible), Korea 1 hour 48 in a 43 country comparison (Plausible).
- City size: Seoul and Gyeonggi weekday commutes 1 hour 30 to 31 minutes in 2024 against Gyeongbuk 50 minutes (Plausible, Statistics Korea via news). Beijing 1 hour 29 minutes in 2018 (Plausible). New York State one way 33.2 minutes in 2024 against the US 27.2 (Plausible). Source: see question 1.

### Inferences
- The constant survived the pandemic: UK total travel fell only 2 percent from 2019 to 2024 while commuting trips fell about a fifth, and the US fall from 70 to 59 minutes a day is the largest move in the series but still leaves total travel inside the 60 to 80 minute band.
- Big dense metros (Seoul, Beijing, New York) sit 10 to 40 minutes a day above their national averages, so density raises travel time at the city level even though national budgets are stable. Remote work and robotaxis both act first in those metros.

### Gaps
- No 2020s peer reviewed test of the Marchetti constant across OECD countries was found. The latest review is 2014.
- No density elasticity of travel time (minutes per unit of density) was found in an openable source.

## Key question 6: net direction and strength of the Place force on paid and free hours

### Takeaway
Over 1 to 5 years the Place force is weak: remote work has been flat at a quarter of paid days since 2022, commute length is back near its 2019 peak, and housing cost pressure adds second jobs at the margin (5.2 to 5.4 percent of the employed). Over 10 to 50 years the force on free hours turns medium as automation converts part of a stable 7 hour a week travel budget into usable time, while the force on paid hours stays weakly positive because about 40 percent of any freed time has flowed back to work.

### Cited Findings
- Reference baselines used below, all from question 1 to 4 sources: US travel 6.9 hours a week per adult in 2025 (Verified rows, derived sum), UK 7.0 hours a week in 2024 (Verified), US work from home 26 percent of paid days in July 2026 (Verified), US multiple jobholding 5.4 percent in 2025 (Verified), US renters cost burdened 49 to 50 percent (Plausible), Waymo 500,000 rides a week in March 2026 (Verified), 40 percent of saved commute time to work (Verified), industry AV timelines pushed 1 to 2 years to about 2029 to 2032 (Plausible, Scenario), ITF urban passenger km up 74 percent 2019 to 2050 (Plausible, Projection).

### Inferences
Horizon by horizon reasoning. Paid hours means paid hours per adult. Free hours means time not in paid work, travel, housework or care.
- 1 year (2027): paid hours weakly up, free hours flat to weakly down. Work from home is flat at 26 percent so no new travel saving. Multiple jobholding rose 0.2 points in a year, which adds under 2 minutes a week per worker. Strength 1. Number: 5.4 percent multiple jobholders in 2025 against 5.2 in 2024.
- 3 years (2029): paid hours weakly up, free hours flat. Employer plans at 1.3 to 1.5 days a week at home match behaviour, so the remote work dividend is already banked. Housing burden at 49 percent of renters keeps the second job pressure on. Strength 1. Number: 26 percent of paid days at home, unchanged since mid 2022.
- 5 years (2031): paid hours weakly up, free hours weakly up. First robotaxi scale in a dozen US metros (Waymo doubling to tenfold a year from 500,000 rides a week), still under 1 percent of trips. Work from home may rise a little as AI widens remote feasible tasks, but no source projects it. Strength 1. Number: 500,000 rides a week against about a billion US trips a day.
- 10 years (2036): paid hours weakly up, free hours weakly to moderately up. Industry surveys put private autonomous car urban pilots at about 2032 and Level 4 trucking at 2029, so by 2036 a visible share of urban car time in early adopter cities can be used for other things. Marchetti says total travel hours stay near 7 a week. Strength 1 to 2, call it 1 for the tile. Number: timelines slipped 1 to 2 years to 2029 to 2032 (Scenario).
- 15 years (2041): paid hours weakly up, free hours moderately up. Older McKinsey work put Level 4 as standard on most new cars near 2040. If half of car time becomes usable and cars are about 60 percent of travel time, about 2 hours a week per adult becomes usable, about 0.8 to work and 1.2 to free time by the 40 percent rule. Strength 2. Number: about 2 hours a week of travel time made usable (Derived scenario).
- 25 years (2051): paid hours weakly up, free hours moderately up. ITF projects urban passenger km up 74 percent 2019 to 2050 and DfT 8 to 54 percent more distance by 2060, so people travel farther in the same hour. Housing costs decide whether that hour is spent on long commutes from cheaper places. Strength 2. Number: urban passenger km plus 74 percent by 2050 (Projection).
- 50 years (2076): paid hours flat to weakly up, free hours moderately up, with wide uncertainty. The only anchor is the 70 minute budget holding for four decades. If automation and remote work remove most driving effort, the travel hour becomes a free or paid hour at the traveller's choice, but travel volume probably rises to fill the budget. Strength 2. Number: 70 minutes a day plus or minus 10 across four decades (Plausible).

### Gaps
- No source models paid hours or free hours per adult as a function of commute, housing cost or automation at these horizons. The strengths are judgement calls built on the numbers above.

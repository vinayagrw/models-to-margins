# Debate map gaps: three LinkedIn claims checked against dated sources

Research notes compiled 2 November 2026. Purpose: the second set of LinkedIn posts for the Time Spent vs Time Saved study is built on a debate map, and a pasted thread makes three claims the repo's research could not settle. Each pass below tries to settle one claim with sources dated 2025 or 2026 where they exist, and says plainly where nothing was found. Method: standard web search first, extended search where the standard result was thin, primary page opened before any Verified tag. Pages that blocked the fetch are named.

Status labels used on every number:
- Verified: I opened the source and read the number there.
- Plausible: the number comes from a secondary source only (news report, search summary, a relay by another publisher). I could not open the primary.
- Projection: a future number from a named author. Never a fact.
- Quote: a person's remark, not a forecast with a method behind it.
- Not found: I looked and could not find a usable number.

Blocked pages this round: goldmansachs.com (403), iea.org (403 on fetch, Cloudflare bot check in the browser), businesswire.com (403), cepr.org (403), emarketer.com (403), metaculus.com (403). The ONS release of 22 October 2026 is a spreadsheet, which I did not download, so its figures are not read.

## Pass 1. "Investment bank analysts say AI will cut the standard workweek to 30 hours by 2045"

### Takeaway
Not found. No dated bank report states a 30 hour or 30 to 32 hour week by any year. The claim is a reshaping of Jamie Dimon's guess of a three and a half day week in 20 to 40 years, made in October 2023 and repeated in November 2025, which lands on 2045 to 2065 and about 28 hours. Bank research dated 2025 and 2026 is about displacement, participation and productivity, not hours per worker.

### Cited findings
- Dimon, 2 October 2023, Bloomberg TV: the next generation will "probably be working three and a half days a week". Quote. Verified (Fortune, 3 October 2023, page updated 24 November 2024). Source: [Fortune, October 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/10/03/jamie-dimon-jpmorgan-chase-ceo-ai-impact-working-week/)
- Dimon, 6 November 2025, America Business Forum, Miami: "My guess is the developed world will be working three and a half days a week in 20, 30, 40 years, and have wonderful lives." Quote. Verified. Source: [Fortune, 6 November 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/11/06/jamie-dimon-ai-will-shorten-work-week-three-and-a-half-days/)
- Dimon, Bloomberg TV, early March 2026: AI may let employees work four days instead of five, with four day or three and a half day weeks over the next three to four decades. Quote, paraphrased by the press. Plausible (the interview itself not opened). Source: [Storyboard18, 8 March 2026](https://storyboard18.com/digital/jamie-dimon-ai-could-shorten-work-week-to-four-days-at-jpmorgan-chase-91524.html)
- Goldman Sachs, note by Joseph Briggs, 22 October 2026: if AI lifts productivity 15 percent in the US and other developed economies, up to 9 percent of workers could be displaced plus about 3 percent from slower hiring, with layoffs likely to wait for a recession. Nothing on hours or the workweek. Projection on jobs, Not found on hours. Verified at Fortune, the Goldman PDF returned 403. Sources: [Fortune, 22 October 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/goldman-sachs-forecast-how-many-jobs-lost-to-ai-9-percent/), [Goldman PDF, blocked](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pdfs/insights/goldman-sachs-research/an-ai-job-apocalypse/report.pdf)
- JPMorgan Asset Management, Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions, 19 October 2026: AI use rises from about 5 percent of tasks today to roughly 40 percent by 2040, US labour productivity 2.2 percent a year, 6 percent of US work hours AI assisted today. No claim on the length of the week. Its downside case is the one where "time savings shift to leisure rather than new tasks". Projection. Verified. Source: [J.P. Morgan Asset Management](https://am.jpmorgan.com/ch/en/asset-management/adv/insights/portfolio-insights/ltcma/a-brighter-productivity-outlook-for-the-ai-era/)
- Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, working paper 2026-07, May 2026 (Karger, Tetlock and others): the median expert expects US labour force participation to fall from 62 to 55 percent by 2050, about half of that from AI. A central bank, not an investment bank, and about who works, not hours per worker. Projection. Verified (abstract page). Source: [Chicago Fed](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2026/2026-07)
- Forecasting Research Institute, Longitudinal Expert AI Panel wave 3, 10 November 2025, 263 experts: 4 percent of US work hours assisted by generative AI in 2025, 8 percent in 2027, 18 percent in 2030. Assisted hours, not hours cut. Projection. Verified. Source: [LEAP wave 3](https://leap.forecastingresearch.org/reports/wave3)
- Bill Gates, March 2025: a two day week from AI abundance, as cited in a Fortune commentary of 21 August 2026 that also lists Dimon and Scaramucci and cites no bank report. Quote. Plausible (the Gates interview not opened). Source: [Fortune, 21 August 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/21/ai-4-day-workweek-fridays-dimon-gates-keynes/)
- Searched and empty: Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Barclays, Deutsche Bank and UBS research on AI and hours per worker, 2023 to 2026, in standard and extended search. Not found.
- Already in the repo: BCG 2050 scenarios of 1,600 to 2,070 hours a year from about 2,100 (Verified, design doc section 24), the Goldman 2023 exposure estimate of about a quarter of US work hours (Plausible, research 03), Autonomy 2023 on 32 hour weeks for 28 percent of UK workers by 2033 (research 13), and the Gemini gap table already marks "30 to 32 hour week by 2045" as unsupported (research 13, row a).

### Line a post could carry
No bank has forecast a 30 hour week. The nearest thing is Jamie Dimon's guess, in 2023 and again in November 2025, of three and a half days a week in 20 to 40 years.

## Pass 2. "Capital and AI infrastructure migrate to places with cheap or loosely regulated power"

### Takeaway
Verified for "power", with a correction to the adjectives. Dated surveys and trackers show power availability as the first siting factor and the US build moving to where power and land can be had. No measured study ties site choice to a low electricity price, and the regulation half runs the other way: the fastest growing state paused permits in 2026 and local moratoriums are multiplying. Labour law arbitrage was not researched, as agreed.

### Cited findings
- Bloom Energy, 2025 Data Center Power Report mid-year update, 17 June 2025, about 100 decision makers surveyed April 2024 to April 2025: 84 percent rank power availability among their top three site selection factors, "power availability is now the leading factor in site selection". 27 percent of facilities expect to run fully on onsite generation by 2030, from 1 percent a year earlier. Utilities quote delivery timelines up to two years longer than developers expect. Vendor survey (a fuel cell maker). Verified. Source: [Bloom Energy release, June 2025](https://www.bloomenergy.com/news/onsite-generation-expected-to-fully-power-27-percent-of-data-center-facilities-by-2030/)
- Bloom Energy, 2026 Data Center Power Report mid-year pulse, June 2026, 156 decision makers surveyed April 2026, 79 percent US based: 51 percent rank power availability in their top three, the next factors (fibre proximity, land, local regulations) sit at 32 and 30 percent. When grid power is the bottleneck, 61 percent would deploy onsite power as their primary strategy and only 12 percent would relocate to a site with power. At least 18 state bills and 86 local moratoriums proposed as of May 2026. Vendor survey. Verified (PDF opened). Sources: [Bloom 2026 mid-year PDF](https://www.bloomenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/power-report-2026-mid-year-pulse-final.pdf), [Bloom release, 15 June 2026](https://www.bloomenergy.com/news/ai-data-center-growth-hinges-on-solving-both-power-constraints-and-community-concerns-bloom-energy-report-finds/)
- JLL, North America Data Center Report year end 2025, 17 February 2026: 35 GW under construction, 64 percent of it beyond the traditional mature markets, Texas alone 6.5 GW. Reason given: "abundant energy resources, ample land availability and business-friendly operating environments", with grid timelines "driving the expansion into frontier markets with more available power resources". Availability, not price. Verified. Source: [JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-us/newsroom/jll-north-america-data-center-report-year-end-2025)
- Baxtel tracker, 13 October 2026: Texas 17.2 GW under construction (32 percent of tracked US capacity) against Virginia 5.5 GW (10 percent), while Virginia still leads operational capacity 12.3 GW to 11.1 GW. "New capacity follows land and power." Texas has paused new data centre permits pending ERCOT and water board audits. Tracker data, no price reason given. Verified. Source: [Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/2026-is-the-year-texas-passes-virginia-in-data-center-capacity)
- Synergy Research, 19 August 2026: the top 20 metro markets hold 60 percent of world hyperscale capacity, 15 of the 20 are in the US, Northern Virginia and Greater Beijing together 17 percent. Dublin, Amsterdam and Singapore fell in the ranking on local constraints. Power availability and cost named as key siting factors, no figure. Verified. Source: [Synergy, August 2026](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/us-rises-again-as-synergy-updates-its-ranking-of-hyperscale-data-center-locations)
- Synergy Research, 23 July 2026: the US will keep "well over half of the world's operational data center capacity" over the next five years. The known pipeline is nearly 1,500 large data centres, almost half in the US, about 45 GW. "Constrained availability of power and rising local concerns over data centers are crimping many new plans." Verified. Source: [Synergy, July 2026](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/synergy-reports-that-us-data-center-capacity-will-continue-to-balloon-despite-increasing-headwinds)
- EPRI, "Powering Intelligence: Updated U.S. Data Center Scenarios", March 2026: data centres take 9 to 17 percent of US electricity by 2030 from 4 to 5 percent today. New capacity appearing in Ohio, Indiana, Pennsylvania and Louisiana "suggests increased prioritization of power access and land availability, particularly for large AI training centers". By 2030 seven more states could see data centres above 20 percent of load. Verified (PDF opened). Source: [EPRI summary PDF](https://restservice.epri.com/publicattachment/97025)
- IEA, Energy and AI, 10 April 2025: the US held 45 percent of world data centre electricity use in 2024, China 25 percent, Europe 15 percent. Nearly half of US capacity sits in five regional clusters and half of US data centres under development are in existing large clusters. 945 TWh by 2030. Plausible (iea.org blocked the fetch and the browser). The cluster line is relayed by Pew, 24 October 2025, which I opened: "Half of the data centers currently being built in the U.S. are part of preexisting large clusters." Sources: [IEA executive summary, blocked](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary), [Pew Research, 24 October 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/24/what-we-know-about-energy-use-at-us-data-centers-amid-the-ai-boom/)
- IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI, 16 April 2026: data centre electricity grew 17 percent in 2025 and 50 percent at AI focused sites, 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, 1.5 percent of world demand rising to about 3 percent. Plausible (page blocked, figures from the IEA page snippet). Source: [IEA, blocked](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)
- Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2025 and 2026: the public pages name "worsening power constraints" and "limited power availability, falling grid reliability, rising costs", with no siting share outside the gated report. Not found for a siting figure. Sources: [Uptime 2025](https://uptimeinstitute.com/resources/research-and-reports/uptime-institute-global-data-center-survey-results-2025), [Uptime 2026](https://uptimeinstitute.com/resources/research-and-reports/uptime-institute-global-data-center-survey-results-2026)
- Price as the driver: Pew, 24 October 2025, lists power availability, zoning, network access and state incentives as the siting factors and treats prices only as a consequence (PJM capacity prices up 9.3 billion dollars, household bills 142 dollars a month in 2024 from 114 in 2014). Verified. A free market think tank claims the top ten data centre states have power prices near the average of the rest. Plausible, not opened. No measured study of site choice against electricity price was found. Not found. Sources: [Pew, as above](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/10/24/what-we-know-about-energy-use-at-us-data-centers-amid-the-ai-boom/), [IER](https://www.instituteforenergyresearch.org/?p=107559)

### Line a post could carry
Power decides where AI gets built: 84 percent of developers put power availability in their top three siting factors in 2025, and 64 percent of the US construction pipeline has left the old hubs. It is power you can get, not cheap power, and the permits are getting tighter, not looser.

## Pass 3. "Where will people spend saved and free time in future"

### Takeaway
Not found. No 2025 or 2026 projection from Pew, Gallup, the ONS, the WEF, McKinsey Global Institute or an academic group puts a figure on how free time will be used in 2030 or later. The forward figures that exist are about work: how many work hours AI will assist, how many people will be in the labour force, and what workers expect to save. The newest measured data points the other way: AI exposed workers gave up leisure after ChatGPT, and most of the work time AI saves is not redirected.

### Cited findings
- ONS, Time use in the UK, released 22 October 2026, 9:30am: two datasets, Wave 9 (2025) and Wave 10 (2026), published as a spreadsheet with no bulletin, and a notice that some wave 10 counts are not yet available. Backward looking and newer than ATUS 2025. Verified that it exists, figures not read (spreadsheet not downloaded). Sources: [ONS release](https://www.ons.gov.uk/releases/timeuseintheuk), [ONS dataset](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/datasets/timeuseintheuk)
- ONS, last published bulletin, 9 November 2023: adults' entertainment, socialising and other free time 3 hours 39 minutes a day, down from 4 hours 17 minutes in 2020. Verified (already in research 31-forces-01, repeated here as the latest ONS figure in words). The May 2024 release reported 3 hours 42 minutes for March 2024. Plausible (search summary). Source: [ONS bulletin, November 2023](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/bulletins/timeuseintheuk/23septemberto1october2023)
- Jiang, Park, Xiao and Zhang, "AI and the Extended Workday", NBER working paper 33536, February 2025, ATUS diaries 2004 to 2023: after ChatGPT, moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of generative AI exposure went with 3.15 more work hours and 3.20 fewer leisure hours a week, the leisure loss concentrated in relaxing, socialising and travel. Direction Verified (NBER abstract opened), magnitudes Plausible (The Register, 21 October 2025, the CEPR column returned 403). Sources: [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33536), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/21/ai_eats_leisure_time)
- BCG, AI at Work 2026, 6 July 2026: 42 percent of employees say AI saves them eight hours a week, and more than half are not using that time for more strategic work. Verified (bcg.com news page). Sample 11,749 workers in 14 markets. Plausible (search summary). Source: [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/news/6july2026-ai-saving-time-workers-strategically)
- Chicago Fed, May 2026: labour force participation 62 to 55 percent by 2050, half from AI (pass 1). The one dated 2050 figure found that bears on free time, and it is about who works, not what anyone does with the hours. Projection. Verified. Source: [Chicago Fed](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2026/2026-07)
- Thomson Reuters, Future of Professionals, 10 July 2024, 2,200 professionals in more than 50 countries: respondents expect AI to save them up to 4 hours a week within a year, 8 within three years and 12 within five, with nothing on what they would do with it. Self reported expectation, 2024. Projection. Plausible (trade press). Source: [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/benefits/ai-expected-to-save-employees-up-to-12-hours-a-week-in-5-years-survey/496570)
- Elon University poll, October 2023: 25 percent of US adults expect AI to improve the way people spend leisure time by 2040 and 26 percent expect it to worsen it. Public expectation, older than preferred. Verified. Source: [Imagining the Digital Future](https://imaginingthedigitalfuture.org/the-impact-of-artificial-intelligence-by-2040/poll-impact-of-ai-on-peoples-personal-lives/)
- Media forecasts: eMarketer puts US adults' traditional TV at 2 hours 14 minutes a day in 2026, the last year linear TV leads. Projection. Plausible (403). IBISWorld holds leisure and sports at about 5.1 hours a day through its five year outlook. Projection. Plausible. A Metaculus crowd forecast for ATUS 2029 leisure sits near 5.4 hours. Plausible (403). Sources: [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-time-spent-with-media-2026), [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/bed/time-spent-on-leisure-and-sports/37), [Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/39276/time-spent-on-activities-in-the-us-in-2029/)
- Searched and empty: Pew Research 2025 and 2026 AI reports (personal life, creativity, chatbot use, no leisure projection), Gallup 2026 (AI use at work, engagement), WEF, McKinsey Global Institute (work hours automatable, 30 percent by 2030 in the 2023 report and 57 percent of US work hours with existing technology in 2026 press, nothing on leisure), AI companion forecasts (market size only, no hours). Not found.

### Line a post could carry
Nobody has published a forecast of where the saved hours will go. The one measured answer points the other way: after ChatGPT, workers in the most exposed jobs put in about three more hours a week and lost about three hours of leisure.

# 05. Dimensions most time-use and AI forecasts ignore

Researched 27 Oct 2026. Labels: Verified (checked against the primary or a direct report this session), Plausible (well known figure, source named, not rechecked this session), Estimate (our own reasoning).

Most AI time forecasts model a salaried knowledge worker in a rich country. That is a small share of the hours people actually live. The ten areas below change the picture.

## 1. Global South and informal work

- Key number: about 58% of the world's workers (nearly 2 billion people) work informally, down from about 61% in the 2018 edition. Verified via WIEGO summary of the ILO 2023 update, ILO page not opened. https://www.ilo.org/publications/women-and-men-informal-economy-statistical-picture-third-edition
- Water: women and girls spend about 200 million hours a day collecting water (UNICEF press release, 29 August 2016, not updated). Verified, URL opened. https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/unicef-collecting-water-often-colossal-waste-time-women-and-girls
- Fuel: about 2.1 billion people still cook with polluting fuels, and wood collection can take hours a day. Plausible. https://www.iea.org/reports/tracking-sdg7-the-energy-progress-report-2024
- Mobile money: M-Pesa access lifted about 194,000 households, or 2% of Kenyan households, out of poverty (Suri and Jack, Science 2016). The figure is contested by a later critique (Bateman, Duvendack and Loubere 2019). Verified via coverage, Science page blocked. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aah5309
- Why it changes the picture: the biggest time sinks for billions of people are pipes, stoves, roads and payments, not email.
- AI-era meaning: a water tap saves more hours than a chatbot for these households. AI helps most here when it rides on mobile money, local language voice and farm advice. Estimate.

## 2. Gender and unpaid care

- Key number: 16.4 billion hours of unpaid care work every day, 76.2% of it done by women (ILO 2018, data from 64 countries covering two thirds of working age people). Verified via ILO press material and search. https://www.ilo.org/publications/major-publications/care-work-and-care-jobs-future-decent-work
- Women do about 2.5 to 3 times more unpaid care and domestic work than men (UN Women). Plausible. https://www.unwomen.org/en/news/in-focus/csw61/redistribute-unpaid-work
- Why it changes the picture: if valued at minimum wage this work is about 9% of world GDP, or USD 11 trillion (ILO 2018, Verified), yet it sits outside every productivity forecast.
- AI-era meaning: AI saves paid office time first. Time saved at work may flow into more paid work, not into sharing care. Without policy the care gap stays. Estimate.

## 3. Ageing and care demand

- Key number: people aged 65 and over will outnumber children under 18 by the late 2070s, and the 80 plus group grows fastest (UN World Population Prospects 2024). Plausible. https://population.un.org/wpp/
- WHO projects a shortfall of about 11 million health workers by 2030 (some WHO pages say 11.1 million), mostly in low and lower middle income countries. Verified. https://www.who.int/health-topics/health-workforce
- Why it changes the picture: care hours rise as worker hours fall. The demand for human time grows in the sectors AI touches least.
- AI-era meaning: the real test is whether AI frees nurse and carer time (notes, scheduling, monitoring), not whether it writes faster reports. Estimate.

## 4. Health and sleep

- Key number: about a third of adults in rich countries report short sleep (under 7 hours) and chronic disease drives most lost healthy years. Plausible. https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/data-research/facts-stats/adults-sleep-facts-and-stats.html
- Why it changes the picture: an hour saved at work is not an hour of rest if it is filled with screens or extra tasks.
- AI-era meaning: track healthy hours and sleep, not just hours saved. Estimate.

## 5. Energy and compute limits

- Key number: data centres used about 415 TWh in 2024 (1.5% of world electricity) and will reach about 945 TWh by 2030, slightly more than Japan uses today. AI-optimised sites more than quadruple. Verified. https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary
- In the US data centres make up nearly half of electricity demand growth to 2030. Verified. Same source.
- Why it changes the picture: AI time savings have a power bill, and grid and chip limits can cap how fast savings spread.
- AI-era meaning: AI time is cheapest where power is cheap. Poor grids in the Global South slow access. Estimate.

## 6. Climate and outdoor work hours

- Key number: heat stress will cut about 2.2% of global working hours by 2030, equal to 80 million full-time jobs and about USD 2.4 trillion. South Asia and West Africa lose about 5%. Agriculture takes 60% of the loss. Verified. https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/increase-heat-stress-predicted-bring-productivity-loss-equivalent-80
- The ILO calls this a conservative estimate based on 1.5 C warming. Verified. Same source.
- Why it changes the picture: climate removes more hours from outdoor workers than AI adds for office workers in the same countries.
- AI-era meaning: net time change can be negative for farm and building workers. AI value here is heat warnings and shift planning. Estimate.

## 7. Inequality of time

- Key number: time poverty is highest for low-income women with children, who often carry paid work plus care (Levy Institute time poverty work). Plausible. https://www.levyinstitute.org/research/the-measurement-of-time-and-income-poverty-ltip
- Early studies show AI gains go to those with access and the tasks that fit it, often in high-income jobs. Plausible. https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/Staff-Discussion-Notes/Issues/2024/01/14/Gen-AI-Artificial-Intelligence-and-the-Future-of-Work-542379
- Why it changes the picture: an average hours-saved number hides who gets the hours.
- AI-era meaning: report saved time by income, gender and country, not as one global figure. Estimate.

## 8. Attention economy and screen time

- Key number: the average internet user spends about 6 hours 38 minutes a day online (GWI data in Digital 2025, January), of which about 3 hours 46 minutes is on mobile. Corrected from 4 to 5 hours on a phone. Verified via secondary reprints, DataReportal page not opened. https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-global-overview-report
- Why it changes the picture: screens already absorb most spare time. Saved time often leaks back into feeds.
- AI-era meaning: AI companions and feeds may claim more hours than AI saves. Time saved is not time gained. Estimate.

## 9. Cognitive offloading

- MIT Media Lab, "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (2025): 54 people in three sessions, only 18 in the fourth session, EEG. The ChatGPT group showed the weakest brain connectivity and worse recall of their own essays. Effects lasted when they later wrote unaided. arXiv preprint (v1 June 2025, v2 December 2025), small sample, no journal publication shown. Verified, URL opened. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872
- Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon survey of 319 knowledge workers (CHI 2025): higher confidence in genAI linked to less critical thinking, higher self-confidence linked to more. Self-reported associations, 936 task examples. Verified, URL opened. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking/
- Why it changes the picture: time saved now may cost skill later, which adds review and rework hours.
- AI-era meaning: count a skill upkeep cost against saved hours, and design AI that asks the user to think first. Estimate.

## 10. What this means overall

- The largest time burdens on Earth (water, fuel, care, heat) sit outside AI forecasts. Estimate.
- AI savings flow mostly to paid office work in rich countries, and are capped by power and eroded by screens and skill loss. Estimate.
- A fair AI time model should add four lines: unpaid hours, climate lost hours, energy cost per saved hour, and who receives the saved hour. Estimate.

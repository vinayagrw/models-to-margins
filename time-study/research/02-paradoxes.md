# Why time-saving technology rarely gives people more free time

Status key. Verified: checked against a source in this session or a primary text. Plausible: well known and widely cited, not rechecked line by line here. Estimate: our own reading or forecast.

Shared thread: saved time is a resource. Firms, markets, social norms and our own wants compete for it. Unless something fixes how the saving is spent (a contract, a law, a norm), it gets spent on more output, more consumption or more coordination.

## 1. Keynes 1930 and the 15-hour week
- Claim: Keynes predicted that by 2030 rich nations would be 4 to 8 times richer and people would work about 15 hours a week. Verified, wording checked against scans of Essays in Persuasion. https://quoteinvestigator.com/2025/02/03/work-week/ (Yale PDF refused connection)
- Evidence: He got the income part roughly right. US and UK real output per head rose about 4 to 6 times since 1930. Plausible. https://ourworldindata.org/economic-growth
- Why it missed: Hours fell from about 3,000 a year per worker in 1870 to roughly 1,700 to 1,800 in the US today, then flattened after about 1980. People took gains as goods, not leisure. Relative wants (status goods) did not saturate, and inequality kept many people needing income. Plausible. https://ourworldindata.org/working-hours
- Strongest counter: Lifetime leisure did rise a lot. Later entry to work, longer retirement and shorter weeks than 1930 mean Keynes was partly right if you count the whole life. Aguiar and Hurst found leisure rose for many US groups 1965 to 2003. Plausible. https://www.nber.org/papers/w12082
- AI prediction: AI gains will show up first as more output and new goods, not shorter weeks, unless policy or contracts lock in hours cuts. Estimate.

## 2. Jevons paradox
- Claim: Making a resource cheaper to use can raise total use of it. Verified (Jevons, The Coal Question, 1865). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox
- Evidence: More efficient steam engines raised coal use. Cheaper lighting raised light consumed by orders of magnitude (Fouquet and Pearson). Plausible. https://www.jstor.org/stable/23297003
- Strongest counter: Full backfire is rare. Most energy rebound studies find partial rebound, often 10 to 60 percent, so some saving survives. Plausible. https://ukerc.ac.uk/publications/the-rebound-effect-an-assessment-of-the-evidence-for-economy-wide-energy-savings-from-improved-energy-efficiency/
- AI prediction: When a report, a draft or a line of code gets cheaper, we will make far more of them. The time cost per unit falls, total time spent on that work may not. Estimate.

## 3. Parkinson's law
- Claim: Work expands to fill the time available. Verified (Economist essay, 1955). https://www.economist.com/news/1955/11/19/parkinsons-law
- Evidence: Parkinson noted British Admiralty staff grew while the fleet shrank. Lab work on deadlines shows people use longer deadlines fully and often finish later (Ariely and Wertenbroch 2002). Plausible. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9280.00441
- Strongest counter: It is a satirical observation, not a law. Plenty of teams bank saved time when goals are fixed and slack is visible. Estimate.
- AI prediction: Without fixed scope or fixed hours, time saved by AI will be absorbed by polish, extra iterations and more review. Estimate.

## 4. Baumol cost disease
- Claim: Sectors with slow productivity growth (care, teaching, live services) get relatively more expensive and take a growing share of spending and labour. Verified. https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.57.3.415 (Baumol 1967)
- Evidence: US education and health prices rose far faster than goods prices for decades. Plausible. https://www.aei.org/carpe-diem/
- Strongest counter: Services that were once stagnant can be automated or remote (recorded music, online learning), so the disease is not permanent. Estimate.
- AI prediction: Time freed in automatable work moves people into human-heavy work that does not speed up. Total hours of society hold steady while the mix shifts. AI could also cure some Baumol sectors, which would be a genuine time gain. Estimate.

## 5. Linder, The Harried Leisure Class (1970)
- Claim: As hourly pay rises, each hour costs more, so people cram more goods into leisure and feel more rushed. Plausible. https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-harried-leisure-class/9780231033954
- Evidence: Higher earners report more time pressure even with similar hours (Hamermesh and Lee, "yuppie kvetch"). Verified. https://www.nber.org/papers/w10186
- Strongest counter: Measured leisure hours did not fall for most groups, so the harried feeling may be about perception and choice overload rather than less time. Plausible.
- AI prediction: If AI raises the value of an hour, people will feel more pressed, not less, and fill leisure with more options. Estimate.

## 6. Solow productivity paradox and the J-curve
- Claim: Solow 1987, "You can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics." Verified. https://www.standupeconomist.com/pdf/misc/solow-computer-productivity.pdf
- Evidence: US productivity grew slowly 1973 to 1995, then jumped 1995 to 2004 as firms reorganised around IT. Brynjolfsson, Rock and Syverson argue general purpose tech needs big unmeasured intangible investment first, so measured productivity dips then rises (the J-curve). Plausible. https://www.nber.org/papers/w25148
- Strongest counter: The 1990s boom was concentrated in IT-producing sectors and faded after 2005 (Gordon). Some argue the gains were smaller than claimed. Plausible. https://www.nber.org/papers/w18315
- AI prediction: Early AI use will consume time (learning, rework, process redesign) before it saves time at scale. Expect a lag of several years. Estimate.

## 7. Time poverty (Ashley Whillans)
- Claim: Most working adults feel they lack time, and buying time raises wellbeing more than buying things. Verified. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1706541114
- Evidence: Whillans and colleagues (PNAS 2017, about 6,000 people in four countries) found spending on time-saving services linked to higher life satisfaction. Gallup data cited by her shows around 80 percent of US workers feel time poor. Plausible. https://hbr.org/2019/01/time-for-happiness
- Strongest counter: The effects are modest and many people still do not buy time when offered, partly from guilt. Time saved is often spent on more work. Plausible.
- AI prediction: AI acts like a time-saving service. It will help only if people consciously spend the saved minutes on rest or relationships, which the research says they usually do not. Estimate.

## 8. Juliet Schor, The Overworked American (1991)
- Claim: US annual hours rose about 160 hours (about a month) between 1969 and 1987. Plausible. https://www.basicbooks.com/titles/juliet-schor/the-overworked-american/9780465054343/
- Evidence: She ties it to the work and spend cycle. Firms prefer long hours for fixed per-worker costs, and workers chase rising consumption norms. Plausible.
- Strongest counter: Time-diary studies (Robinson and Godbey, Aguiar and Hurst) found free time stayed flat or rose. Schor used survey-based hours, which tend to overstate. Plausible. https://www.nber.org/papers/w12082
- AI prediction: Even if AI boosts output, firm-level incentives favour keeping hours steady and taking the gain as output. Estimate.

## 9. Hedonic adaptation
- Claim: People quickly get used to gains, so new comfort or free time stops feeling special. Plausible. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonic_treadmill
- Evidence: Brickman, Coates and Janoff-Bulman 1978 on lottery winners. Easterlin paradox on income and happiness. Plausible. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/690806/
- Strongest counter: Adaptation is incomplete for some changes (unemployment, disability, commuting). Lucas and Diener showed set points can move. Plausible.
- AI prediction: Today's AI speed-ups will become tomorrow's baseline expectation. Deadlines and output targets will reset upward. Estimate.

## 10. Email and meeting load after digital tools
- Claim: Cheaper communication raised its volume, so coordination now eats much of the workday. Verified.
- Evidence: Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025 reports about 275 interruptions a day, roughly one every two minutes, plus about 117 emails and 153 Teams messages a day. Verified (also Axios, which gives one ping every 1.75 minutes). https://www.axios.com/2025/06/17/microsoft-remote-work-meetings https://www.techspot.com/news/108343-microsoft-study-finds-infinite-workday-hurting-productivity.html
- Evidence: Meeting time rose sharply after 2020 (Microsoft reported weekly meeting time up about 250 percent from Feb 2020 to 2022). Plausible. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index
- Strongest counter: Some meeting growth replaced travel and office chatter, and async tools let some teams cut meetings. Plausible.
- AI prediction: AI that writes email and summarises meetings cuts cost per message, so volume goes up. A Jevons effect on communication. Estimate.

## 11. Four-day week trials
- Claim: Cutting hours with no pay cut can keep output steady, which shows hours were partly slack.
- UK 2022: 61 firms, about 2,900 staff, June to December 2022. 56 firms (92 percent) kept going, 18 made it permanent. Quits fell 57 percent, revenue broadly flat (Verified). Sick days fell about two thirds (Plausible, not confirmed this pass). https://www.npr.org/2023/02/21/1158507132/uk-study-companies-four-day-workweek
- Iceland 2015 to 2019: about 2,500 public workers (just over 1 percent of the workforce) moved to 35 to 36 hours with no pay cut. Output held or improved, and 86 percent of the workforce now works shorter hours or has the right to. Verified. https://autonomy.work/portfolio/icelandsww/
- Strongest counter: Firms self-selected, no random control. Revenue data came from a subset. Iceland mostly cut hours by about 1 to 4 a week, not a full day, and some sites added overtime. Plausible.
- AI prediction: This is the one lever with real evidence that saved time becomes free time. It works because the hour cut is fixed first, which forces the saving into leisure. Estimate.

## Bottom line for AI
Every mechanism above says the default is to spend AI gains on more output, more communication and higher expectations. Only fixed rules on hours, like four-day week contracts, reliably turn efficiency into free time. Estimate.

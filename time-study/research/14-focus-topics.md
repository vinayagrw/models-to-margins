## 1. Time rebound (Jevons for time)

What we have.
- 02-paradoxes.md lines 14 to 17: Jevons paradox for coal and energy, with the partial rebound range of 10 to 60 percent.
- 02-paradoxes.md line 67: an estimate that AI email and meeting tools raise message volume.
- 07-business-models.md lines 29 to 32: Jevons for compute demand, not for human time.
- 11-persona-panel.md line 139: a panelist notes the AI rebound case is still open.

What is missing.
- No measured rebound for time itself. We have the idea, not a number.
- No historical case of a tool that cut task time and then grew task count (email, spreadsheets, word processing, photocopiers).
- No method to split a time saving into kept, reinvested and lost.

Key figures.
- Energy rebound is usually partial, often 10 to 60 percent, so some saving survives. Plausible, already cited in 02 line 17 (UKERC report, not reopened).
- Jevons, The Coal Question (1865), first made the case that cheaper use raised total coal use. Plausible, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox (not reopened).
- No primary time rebound figure was found in this pass. This is the core gap to fill, for example with email volume per worker series or Microsoft Work Trend Index meeting counts.

## 3. Time poverty and commuting (Marchetti constant)

What we have.
- 01-history.md lines 20 to 21: Marchetti constant and a commute study cited.
- 01-history.md line 70: faster transport bought distance, not time.
- 01-history.md lines 78 to 79 and 11-persona-panel.md line 58: commute minutes before 1950 rest on theory, not surveys.

What is missing.
- No survey series for commute time across decades (US Census journey to work, UK National Travel Survey).
- Nothing on remote work as a break in the constant since 2020.
- Weak link to business decisions beyond office policy.

Key figures.
- People spend roughly the same average time travelling to work wherever they live. In two Chinese cities, commute time sits slightly above that constant and longer distance commuters settle at a new, higher steady level. Verified (abstract), https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.03952
- Marchetti's constant of about one hour a day of travel. Plausible, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchetti%27s_constant (not reopened).
- Ramey and Francis long series on work and leisure. Plausible, https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~vramey/research/Century_of_Work_and_Leisure.pdf (page would not load).

## 4. Felt time pressure and attention (time famine)

What we have.
- 02-paradoxes.md lines 32 to 35: Linder, The Harried Leisure Class, and the "yuppie kvetch" idea.
- 05-missed-dimensions.md lines 57 to 61: about 6 hours 38 minutes a day online, saved time leaks into feeds.
- 08-behaviour-horizons.md lines 72 to 168: attention rows in the scenario tables.
- 11-persona-panel.md lines 98 to 100: time pressure surveys named as a signal to watch.

What is missing.
- No long series of how rushed people feel (for example Pew or General Social Survey "always rushed" items).
- No link between screen hours and felt pressure.
- Fixed 2026-10-27. The link in 02 line 34 (NBER w9387) points to a trade data paper, not Hamermesh and Lee. The correct paper is w10186.

Key figures.
- Higher income adults report more time stress even when they spend the same hours on paid and household work. Working wives report the most stress. Verified, https://www.nber.org/papers/w10186
- Women report more time stress than men at equal total work hours, across Australia, Canada, Germany, Korea and the US. Verified, same source.
- The average internet user is online about 6 hours 38 minutes a day. Plausible, https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-global-overview-report (not reopened).

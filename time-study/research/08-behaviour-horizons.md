# 08. Human behaviour and time use, 2027 to 2076

Research date: 27 October 2026. Baseline year: 2026.

Labels used in this file:

- **Verified**: checked against a named source, URL given.
- **Plausible**: reported by credible secondary sources or widely cited, not checked line by line against the primary.
- **Estimate**: our own number or inference from the evidence.
- **Projection**: all scenario content. These are not facts. They are structured guesses.

## 1. Evidence base

### 1.1 Past adoption curves

| Technology | What happened | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

| Electricity and productivity | Factory productivity gains lagged adoption by decades because plants had to be redesigned around the electric motor (Paul David, "The Dynamo and the Computer", AER Papers and Proceedings, May 1990). | Smartphone (US adults) | 35% in 2011, 91% in the latest (2026) survey. Mass reach in about 10 to 13 years. | Verified | https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/ |
| Generative AI | ChatGPT reached a reported 100 million users in about two months in 2023, faster than any prior consumer app. | Plausible | https://www.reuters.com/technology/chatgpt-sets-record-fastest-growing-user-base-analyst-note-2023-02-01/ |

Takeaway (Estimate): software that rides on existing devices spreads in years. Changes to how work, schooling and institutions are organised follow the electricity pattern and take decades. Behaviour in the home changes faster than behaviour inside institutions.

### 1.3 Named scenarios we draw on

| Author | Idea | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| John Maynard Keynes, 1930 | "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren": a 15 hour week by about 2030. Incomes rose as forecast, hours did not fall as forecast. | Verified | http://www.econ.yale.edu/smith/econ116a/keynes1.pdf |
| Daron Acemoglu, 2024 | "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI": about 0.7% total factor productivity gain over ten years. The slow case. | Verified | https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487 |
| Goldman Sachs, 2023 | Up to 7% global GDP lift over ten years, about two thirds of US jobs exposed to some automation. | Plausible | https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/generative-ai-could-raise-global-gdp-by-7-percent |
| Erik Brynjolfsson | Productivity J curve. Gains appear late after organisational change. | Plausible | https://www.nber.org/papers/w25148 |
| AI 2027 (Kokotajlo and others) | Very fast takeoff to transformative AI by about 2027 to 2030. The fast tail. | Verified | https://ai-2027.com/ |
| Daniel Susskind, "A World Without Work" (2020) | Work shrinks over decades, the hard problem becomes meaning and distribution. | Plausible | https://www.danielsusskind.com/a-world-without-work |
| IMF, 2024 | About 40% of jobs worldwide exposed, about 60% in advanced economies, about 26% in low income countries. | Verified | https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity |
| Robert Putnam, "Bowling Alone" (2000) | Civic association declined with TV. A precedent for screen time crowding out civic time. | Plausible | https://bowlingalone.com/ |

## 2. How to read the scenarios

- **Slow**: AI follows the electricity path. Big consumer use, small institutional change, productivity near Acemoglu.
- **Base**: smartphone speed at home, electricity speed at work. Gains near Goldman and Brynjolfsson over 10 to 15 years.
- **Fast**: capability jumps on the AI 2027 path, with firms reorganising quickly.
- **Confidence** is our confidence that the true outcome falls inside the slow to fast range, not that base is right. It falls with distance (Estimate).

All scenario cells below are **Projection**.

## 4. Cross cutting notes (Estimate)

1. Keynes was right on income and wrong on hours. People took gains as consumption, not leisure. Expect the same bias unless policy shifts it.
2. Use is already mostly personal (73% non work in ChatGPT). Home life changes before work life.
3. Automation share is rising fastest in enterprise API use. That is the leading indicator for job change.
4. Public mood is wary (Pew). That slows adoption in schools, health and government.
5. The Global South has lower exposure (IMF 26%) but also lower access. The main risk is loss of outsourcing jobs before local gains arrive.

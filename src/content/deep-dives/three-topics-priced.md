<section class="mwf-exec" aria-label="Executive summary">
<p class="mwf-exec-k">Executive summary</p>
<p class="mwf-exec-lead">Fourteen topics recur across eight reports. Scored on who pays, how much, and what forces action, three lead: workflows that pay, AI decisions as liability, and the power and labour needed to build AI data centers. The largest market of the three is the one a software or services firm can reach least.</p>
<ol class="mwf-exec-list">
<li><b>Workflows that pay</b><span>The spend is real and has owners: $120.9B of customer service wages and $86.0B of insurance loss expenses in the United States. One independent study measures a gain. Vendors decide for themselves when a unit of work is complete.</span></li>
<li><b>AI decisions as liability</b><span>The duties have dates and the losses so far are small. Analysts size governance software at $275M to $492M for 2026. No source states what one regulated firm spends.</span></li>
<li><b>Data-center power, people and debt</b><span>The largest numbers on the page. The money goes to equipment makers, electrical contractors and lenders. Five of seven simulated roles passed on it.</span></li>
</ol>
<p class="mwf-exec-ask"><span>The decision this asks of you</span>Pick the topic that matches a budget you can reach. For most software and services firms that is the first one, and the first step is a client's own cost per completed unit.</p>
</section>

## How the three were chosen

Eight reports were coded for recurring topics, which gave 14. An earlier ranking scored how widely each topic appeared. This one scores money. Each topic gets 1 to 5 on five tests: a named budget owner pays now, the size of the cost or spend, breadth across three or more report series, a dated trigger in the next 24 months, and strength of evidence.

The fourth-placed topic, model labs selling directly into industries, tied the second and third on points. I took the grid topic because its payer and its problem differ most from the other two, and merged the financing chain into it because both depend on the same build schedules. The sections below run in the order the simulated roles preferred, which puts workflows first.

<p class="mwf-take"><span>What this means</span>The choice between third and fourth place was a judgment call. A reader who sells software to platforms should weigh the labs topic in its place.</p>

## Objections

These are the strongest arguments against the page as a whole.

<div class="mwf-obj">
<p class="mwf-mono">Objection 1</p>
<p class="mwf-q">The topics come from a narrow base.</p>
<p>Eight reports from six series were read, of 17 available. The same series chose what to cover, so the list reflects their scope.</p>
<p>This stands. The ranking is of what these reports surfaced. It is not a ranking of every business problem.</p>
</div>

<div class="mwf-obj">
<p class="mwf-mono">Objection 2</p>
<p class="mwf-q">The rows in each chart are not comparable.</p>
<p>A wage bill, an expense line, an analyst forecast and a funding total are different kinds of number.</p>
<p>Correct, which is why each row carries a mark and nothing is added across rows. The charts show order of magnitude and who is the source.</p>
</div>

<div class="mwf-obj">
<p class="mwf-mono">Objection 3</p>
<p class="mwf-q">The role verdicts are a model talking to itself.</p>
<p>Seven simulated roles are not seven buyers.</p>
<p>This stands. They were given only the verified figures and told to argue against each topic. They are a structured reading of the evidence and no more than that.</p>
</div>

<div class="mwf-obj">
<p class="mwf-mono">Objection 4</p>
<p class="mwf-q">Workflows may be first only because they are easiest to count.</p>
<p>Wages and expense lines are published. Liability and grid costs are harder to see.</p>
<p>Partly right. The liability section could not find what one firm spends, and absence of a figure lowered its standing. A disclosed budget would change the order.</p>
</div>

<p class="mwf-take"><span>What this means</span>Read the order as provisional. The second and third objections are limits of the method and are not answered here.</p>

## What to do next

1. If you sell software or services, start with workflows. Ask one client for its own cost per completed unit in customer service or claims before quoting anything.
2. If you run risk at a regulated firm, list the models that make final decisions and check the insurance renewal for an AI exclusion.
3. If you lend to or invest in data centers, list the positions with termination or deferral clauses tied to completion dates.

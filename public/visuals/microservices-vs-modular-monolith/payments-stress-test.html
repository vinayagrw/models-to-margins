Four production platforms, four different answers

← One deployable
~2,800 services →
Adyen
One Java + PostgreSQL platform, explicitly not microservices
A modular monolith runs a real acquiring hot path at top-10-PSP scale, hundreds of billions of EUR per year.
Stripe
50M-line Ruby monorepo core, services at the edges
The core stayed cohesive through a decade of hypergrowth. Radar won its ~100ms fraud budget in-process, network hops ate it at 2–5ms each.
Lithic
Coarse services, every one active-active multi-region
A service-oriented hot path is viable with few hops, replication and request hedging doing the availability work.
Monzo
~2,800 Go microservices in a monorepo + a stand-in stack
The maximal pole works, at the price of a funded platform org. And even Monzo keeps a radically simple independent path for the moment cards must work.
The four forces that decide the shape (none appear in generic comparisons)
Force 01
The hop tax is a tail, not a mean
99.99%⁵ → 99.95%
Five sequential p99-jittery hops blow a ~250ms budget even when averages look fine. Sequential dependencies multiply availability risk.
Force 02
The ledger cannot be a saga
No atomicity, no split
Sagas explicitly cannot give atomicity. Splitting the ledger is where payment microservice migrations actually fail.
Force 03
PCI scope follows access
One vault, tokens elsewhere
A mesh inside the compliance boundary multiplies audit evidence. Shopify, proudly monolithic, extracted exactly this one enclave.
Force 04
Fraud ML is the natural seam
Fail-open, own cadence
Advisory, read-mostly, different stack. The textbook extraction even inside a monolith strategy. Stripe, Adyen, and Visa all do it.

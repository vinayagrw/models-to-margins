# LinkedIn draft: payee fraud story line (not posted)

In early 2025, someone got into a supplier's account in the City of Baltimore's payment system and changed one thing: the bank details.

Staff approved the change without checking the documents. Two payments, $1.5 million, went out on time, to the fraudster.

The city got one back. $803,384 never came home.

The payment network worked perfectly. It sent the money exactly where it was told. The failure happened earlier, at "who are we paying?"

This is not rare. 74% of US organizations faced email compromise attempts or losses in 2025 (AFP). The FBI logged $3.05 billion in reported losses.

For business payments, the loss usually stays with the payer if the bank followed procedure.

Three controls to check on Monday:
1. Call back on a number you already hold for any bank detail change.
2. Hold the first payment to new details.
3. Let software flag risky changes. A person releases the money.

Which one does your team skip?

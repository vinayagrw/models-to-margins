# Why surveys say 88% and the Census Bureau says about 20%

Checked on 2026-10-25. Every page below was opened and read. No figure comes from a search snippet.

Short answer: the two numbers count different things. McKinsey counts survey respondents, mostly senior and many at large firms, who say their organisation uses AI regularly somewhere. The Census Bureau counts every US employer business with equal weight, most of them small, and asks whether the business used AI in the last two weeks.

## Source notes

### US Census Bureau BTOS

- Wording before the change. The archived questionnaire (Content V3, revised 1 Aug 2024) asks whether the business used AI "in producing goods or services" in the two-week period. A footnote added 23 Oct 2023 defines AI as computer systems that do tasks needing human intelligence.
- Wording after the change. Data from period 202524 onward (collection from 17 Nov 2025) ask about use in any of the business's functions. The national file's question text adds examples: AI data analysis, generative AI for marketing, design or coding, virtual agents for customer service, forecasting or pricing, and computer vision for quality control. The Census story says the old wording did not cover tasks such as drafting emails.
- Label change. The July 2026 questionnaire still says Artificial Intelligence. The national file published 22 Oct 2026 and the current questionnaire call it Super Intelligence or SI, formerly AI. The file notes it is published in line with Executive Order 14434. The question scope is unchanged.
- Who is surveyed. All US employer businesses, single and multi-location, excluding farms, railroads, the Postal Service, religious and civic groups, private households, public administration and some financial vehicles (methodology, version 5, October 2026).
- Sample and frequency. About 1.2 million businesses a year, six biweekly panels of about 200,000, each panel asked once every 12 weeks. Voluntary. New sample every year.
- Weighting. Sample weights with two nonresponse adjustments, one by state and size class, one by sector and size class. Size classes are 4 or fewer staff, 5 to 19, and 20 or more. Each business counts once whatever its size. The published share divides by every business that answered the question, so the "do not know" group sits in the denominator.
- Recall window. Last two weeks.
- Gap. No data for 6 Oct to 16 Nov 2025 because of the federal funding lapse.
- Series on the new wording. 17.3% (Nov 2025), 19.8% (period ending 3 May 2026), 24.6% (period ending 4 Oct 2026). Expected use in the next six months is 28.6% in the latest period.
- Coming change. The current questionnaire adds a separate question on whether employees used generative AI for work tasks. The 22 Oct 2026 national file has no estimates for it yet.
- AI supplement. Pooled from all six panels, 17 Nov 2025 to 8 Feb 2026, published 23 Apr 2026. The Minneapolis Fed size and sector splits come from it. The supplement table itself was not opened, only the methodology note describing it.

### Minneapolis Fed article

What it adds beyond census.gov pages opened here:
- States plainly that the broader wording drives much of the jump from about 10% to 20%.
- Firm-size split from the supplement: 30% at 250 or more staff, 17% under 20 staff. This differs from the Census story's 37% for 250 or more because the periods differ (supplement pooled Nov 2025 to Feb 2026, story period ending 3 May 2026).
- Sector spread: information and professional services near 4 in 10, manufacturing about 12%, several sectors under 10%.
- Ninth District about 18%, Minnesota 20%.
- About 96% of AI-using firms reported no headcount change in the prior six months.
- It gives no sample size or weighting and does not compare BTOS with McKinsey.

### McKinsey State of AI

- Headline. The 2026 report says nearly nine in ten. Exhibit 1 shows 89% for 2026 and 88% for 2025.
- Definition. The Exhibit 1 footnote says the 2025 and 2026 definition is "regular use of AI in at least 1 function". Earlier years used other definitions (core part of the business in 2017, at least one capability embedded in 2018 to 2019, adopted in at least one function from 2020), so the long trend line mixes definitions.
- Unit. Every chart is "% of respondents". It is a share of people who answered, not a share of firms.
- Respondents. Self-selected online participants from all regions, industries, sizes, functions and tenures. Among respondents who use AI in their own roles, 654 were C-level, 424 executives or senior managers, 217 midlevel managers and 195 individual contributors.
- Firm size. 36% at firms above $1 billion revenue (2026), 38% (2025). In 2026, 595 respondents at $1 billion or more and 1,035 at smaller firms answered the follow-on questions.
- Weighting. By each respondent nation's share of global GDP. There is no weighting to the number of firms by size or sector.
- Recall window. None. "Regular use" is not tied to a period.
- mckinsey.com was opened for 2026 and shows the date 25 August 2026, which settles the open date question in VR2-23.

### Stanford AI Index 2026

- The 88% is McKinsey's 2025 figure. Every adoption chart in section 4.3 carries "Source: McKinsey & Company Survey, 2025".
- The chapter says the results are self-reported and directional rather than comprehensive.
- Regional figures from the same source: Europe 91%, North America 90% (2025).
- The chapter drops McKinsey's word "regular" when describing the 88%.
- Mismatch to note: the web summary says generative AI is used in at least one function at 70% of organisations. The chapter PDF says 79%. Use the PDF figure.
- Neither the page nor the chapter mentions the Census BTOS.

## Why the two figures differ

- Different unit. McKinsey reports the share of survey respondents. The Census Bureau reports the share of all US employer businesses, each counted once.
- Different firms. Most US employer businesses are small, and BTOS weights them in proportion to their numbers. McKinsey draws more than a third of answers from firms above $1 billion revenue and does not weight by firm count.
- Size is not the whole story. Even among the largest US firms, BTOS finds 30% to 37% use AI, far below McKinsey's 89%. Who answers and how the question is asked also matter.
- Different respondents. McKinsey hears mainly from executives who opted into an online survey about AI, so firms already using AI are likely overrepresented. BTOS asks a random sample of all businesses.
- Different question. McKinsey asks about regular use anywhere in the organisation with no time limit. BTOS asks about use in the last two weeks, and before November 2025 counted only use in producing goods or services.
- The Census wording change matters. Broadening from production to any business function roughly doubled the BTOS share, from about 10% to about 20%. Comparisons across that date are not like for like.
- Different geography and timing. McKinsey is global and weighted by national GDP, with Europe at 91% and North America at 90%. BTOS covers US businesses only, and its latest reading is 24.6% for the two weeks to 4 October 2026.
- Pick by question. Use BTOS for how widespread AI is across the US business population. Use McKinsey for how AI is used inside large organisations that engage with AI surveys.

# R2: Independent surveys and studies on enterprise frontier AI adoption challenges

Strand: R2, measured numbers from surveys, studies and reports. Compiled 2026-10-25. Web search accuracy is out of scope.

How to read this file

- Rung uses the evidence ladder: 1 audited or peer reviewed, 2 primary record, 3 preprint or revealed behaviour, 4 deployment scale, 5 credible secondary or independent survey, 6 self-report (consulting or investor run), 7 vendor claim or unopened.
- I put consulting surveys at rung 6 because the firm sells the fix. I put vendor-run surveys at rung 7 because the vendor sells the product that answers the problem. Gartner, academic and statistical sources sit at rung 5 or better.
- Text in quotation marks is verbatim from the opened page, one quote per source, under 15 words. All other numbers are close paraphrases of the page wording.
- FLAG marks a survey run by a firm that sells the fix.
- Theme tags: ROI, PILOT, COST, GOV, SEC, SHADOW, PI (prompt injection), DATA, SKILLS, TRUST, LOCKIN, AGENTS, INTEG, MEASURE, PEOPLE, ENERGY.

## 3. Contradictions between studies

1. ROI share swings from 5% to 75% depending on the bar.
   - NANDA says 95% of organizations get zero return (July 2025, 153 surveys and 52 interviews).
   - Wharton GBK says three in four leaders see positive returns (October 2025).
   - Gartner keynote says 58% see some positive value, but the Gartner CIO survey in the same week says only 13% see significant or transformational value (October 2026).
   - McKinsey finds 37% with any EBIT impact and 6% high performers (August 2026). BCG finds 48.5% creating value (September 2026). Accenture finds 25% with widespread and sustained value (October 2026). WRITER finds 29% with significant GenAI ROI.
   - Reading: the gap is mostly definition. "Some value" lands near 60% to 75%. "Significant" or EBIT-material value lands near 5% to 25%. Every one of these is self-report. None is audited.
2. Individual productivity versus enterprise value. McKinsey says 80% report better individual productivity and Accenture says 82%, yet EBIT impact is flat at 37% (McKinsey). Workday says nearly 40% of time saved goes to rework and only 14% get consistent net gains. METR measured a 19% slowdown in 2025 and could not get a reliable answer in 2026.
3. Adoption base rates differ by a factor of four. Stanford and McKinsey report 88% to 89% of organizations using AI. Ramp revealed spend shows more than half of businesses paying for AI. The US Census shows about 20% of businesses using AI in any function. IBM says roughly one in four workers regularly use AI. The first two survey large, self-selected respondents. Census is a federal sample of all firm sizes.
4. Build or buy. NANDA says external partnerships reach deployment twice as often as internal builds and Menlo says 76% of use cases are bought. McKinsey says 32% decided against buying software because coding agents let them build it.
5. Cost falling or rising. Ramp shows the effective token price down 41% since March 2026. Yet 20% of firms say costs constrain use (McKinsey), 51% of CIOs expect AI to raise TCO and 58% feel more cost pressure (Gartner). Lower unit prices are being outrun by volume and by agent workloads.
6. Workforce cuts. WRITER says 69% of companies already make AI-linked layoffs. McKinsey says last year's expected cuts were about double the cuts that happened. Accenture says 61% of leaders expect hiring to rise. Gartner says 40% of CIOs expect IT headcount to grow in 2027.
7. Agent maturity. Salt says 86.4% of its respondents run at least one agent. McKinsey says 40% of large firms are scaling agents. Accenture says 20% deploy agents across functions. Stanford says agent deployment is single digits per function and Menlo says 16% of deployments are true agents. Definitions of "agent" are not shared.
8. Agreement worth noting: three independent sources land on the same agent governance number. Gartner (21%), Deloitte (21%) and BCG (5% with the full set of controls) all show controls trailing deployment.
